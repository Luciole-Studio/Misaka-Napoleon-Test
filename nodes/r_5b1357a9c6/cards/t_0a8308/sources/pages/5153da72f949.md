[# #Exhibit of the Month](https://www.nationalmuseum.md/en/exhibit_of_the_month/) [>>>](https://www.nationalmuseum.md/en/news/arhive/september_2026/)
The Chișinău Diocesan Girls' School opened its doors on May 3, 1864, addressing a pressing need: in Bessarabia there were no institutions dedicated to the education of clergy's daughters. According to the 1864 circular, teaching was conducted in the Romanian language, a provision applied throughout the diocese. The school's first headmistress was Anastasia Rizo, of noble origin, invited by Archbishop Antonie.

The curriculum, approved by the Ministry of Public Education and the Chișinău Diocese, combined theoretical subjects with practical training. Students studied religion, reading and writing in Romanian, Slavonic, and Russian, arithmetic and exercises in calculation, world history and geography. The program was complemented by church singing and practical skills such as sewing, embroidery, knitting, as well as household management - baking bread and prosphora, preparing meals and preserves, caring for livestock, and cultivating gardens.

The composite, taken in 1911 at the studio of Z. N. Sumovski in Chișinău, depicts 29 graduates and 13 teachers. At the center are portraits of Bishop Serafim (Ciciagov), headmistress N. R. Mihelson, teacher V. E. Bejerian, and priests F. Ghiliarov and P. Jurikas. Among the teaching staff was Paraskovia Vasilievna Cuciurenko, a calligraphy teacher for 37 years, who in 1911 became senior educator and assistant headmistress. For her dedication, she was awarded the gold medal "For Long Service."

On the reverse of the vignette is a label from the store and workshop of frames, paintings, mirrors, and window glass owned by A. D. Kon'gova, Chișinău, Gogol Street no. 37, near the State Bank.

This graduation composite was donated at the end of 2025 to the National Museum of History of Moldova by the Dadiștian family from Drochia.

# The role of grain exports in external commerce of Bessarabia (1812-1830)

### Tyragetia, serie nouă, vol. IV [XIX], nr. 2, Istorie. Muzeologie

In this paper the role of grain exports in commerce of Bessarabia, in the period between its annexation (1812) and the retrenchment of the Dniester custom and sanitary cordon (1830) is examined. Based on archival and published sources the author shows the main directions of the exports, the regulation of the grain exports by provincial administration and the role of commercial capital in the foreign grain trade of the Bessarabia.

The main conclusions show that the exports were practiced mainly through Ismail, Reni and Odessa seaports, even though Bessarabia was a border province and had also land customs. The grain commerce was held by Greek and Armenian merchants that had strong relationships with commercial bourgeois from main European city-ports. The augmentation of grain exports from Bessarabia was curbed by intense regulation, because the province was used as quarter for important military troops.
