   The sudden and apparently mysterious death of Crown Prince Carl
August soured relations between Denmark-Norway and Sweden, while
the Swedes were thrown into yet another domestic political crisis.
Although he had kept a low profile for most of his brief tenure as crown
prince, Carl August had proved to be an acceptable and unifying figure
among the various political factions in the Swedish Riksdag, including
what was still the powerful Gustavian party that supported the heridi-
tary rights of the deposed King Gustav IV Adolph. His death, however,
triggered new conflicts about the future course of Sweden’s domestic
and foreign policy and, not least, about the question of succession.


The Second Turning Point:
The Second Crisis of Swedish Succession

The Swedish succession crisis of 1810 – the second in only a year – marks
a turning point in Scandinavian history, and its importance cannot be
overestimated. Not only did it determine the course of Scandinavian
politics during the years that followed right up until the twentieth cen-
tury, it also provides a clear example of what is known in counterfactual
history writing as decisive or open historical situations, when history
could potentially have taken a completely different course.18 In the case
of the issue of Swedish succession in 1810, the crisis revolved as much
around those who were not elected as it did around the final choice,
namely the French marshal Jean Baptiste Bernadotte.19 In other words,
if someone other than Bernadotte had been elected – which was indeed
the most likely outcome – Scandinavian history would most certainly
have taken a completely different course.
