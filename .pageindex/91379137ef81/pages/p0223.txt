  The Peace of Kiel was signed on 14 January 1814. This was, in fact,
two separate peace treaties, one with Great Britain and one with
Sweden. In the first treaty the Danish king surrendered the island of
Heligoland, which had been occupied since 1807, to Britain, while the
British returned Denmark’s overseas colonies. The second peace treaty
obliged Frederick VI to cede all of Norway to the Swedish king in return
for Swedish Pomerania, the principality of Rügen and one million rix-
dollars. At the same time the Swedes placed Norway under an obligation
to pay its share of the former Danish-Norwegian state’s national debt.
Finally, it was explicitly stated in the treaty that Denmark would retain
the old Norwegian dependencies in the North Atlantic: Iceland, the
Faeroes and Greenland, simply because Charles John was not interested
in anything but ‘mainland Norway’.63
