   On the other hand, the crisis and the war also created friction between
the various regions of the country and even among local populations as
some profited, rather than suffered, from war and occupation. In spite
of complaints about their ‘foul and avaricious mercenary spirit’, the
wealthy farmers of Hedmarken, the area of Norway richest in corn and
very rarely subject to harvest failures, promptly seized the opportunity
offered by the war to profit shamelessly at the expense of those areas
where there was a dearth of corn, and at the expense of the soldiers.
For his part, Prince Christian August accused the people of Trøndelag
of stockpiling corn that had been acquired in Arkhangelsk in Russia
instead of sending it to the most needy parts of Norway. Therefore, soli-
darity and concord in Norway during the war years of 1808–09 should
not be exaggerated, even though in the long term the war would little by
little help to create a heightened sense of fellowship and independence
among many Norwegians, at least in their opposition to Denmark.67


The Fall of Gustav IV Adolph and an Opportunity
for Scandinavia

For all practical purposes Sweden had lost Finland to Russia by the
beginning of 1809, once the Swedish army had retreated to Sweden
proper. Far from being out of danger, however, the Swedes found
themselves in an even more desperate situation. Tsar Alexander
of Russia had assured Frederick VI that he would take the war into
Sweden and expected Denmark-Norway to assist him in doing so.68
Consequently, Frederick VI began to make new plans to invade
Scania – but now without the help of allied troops – while at the same
time the Norwegian army received orders to make new preparations
to invade western Sweden.
