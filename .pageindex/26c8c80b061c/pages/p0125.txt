

When  the  depreciation  in  the  Money  price  of  Cotton,  and  other  Wove  Fabrics  has  been  adverted  to,  it  has  been  usual  to  ascribe  their  depreciation  to  Foreign 
Competition ; but  the  depreciation  in  the  Monet/  Price  of  Iron,  is  equal  to  the  depreciation  of  Wove  Fabrics : whilst  for  the  depreciation  of  Iron,  the  cause  of  Foreign 
Competition  cannot  be  assigned  : the  only  Country  in  the  World,  which  now  mat  es  a sufficient  quantity  of  Iron  for  its  use  is  Sweden,  where  it  is  made  at  a cost  ex- 
ceeding a half  more  than  what  it  is  made  for  in  England  ; the  depression  in  the  Money  price  of  Iron  must  therefore  be  sought  for,  in  other  causes  than  Foreign 
Competition. ! ! The  strongest  test  of  contrast  to  which  the  depreciation  of  Iron,  and  other  products  of  labor  can  be  subjected,  is  Money  Property:  in  1815  £53,819,780 
Money  was  borrowed,  for  which  £3,083,62!  of  Annuity  was  granted,  equal  to  67,621  Tons  of  Iron  at  £45  12  3 Ton,  the  average  price  at  which  Exported  in  that 
Year : whilst  in  1829  146,316  Tons  were  required,  to  pay  the  same  amount  of  Annuity ; a disproportion  of  more  than  2 for  I.  Great  as  is  this  disproportion,  it  is  near 
ly  doubly  so,  when  viewed  in  reference  to  the  Money  price  of  the  Slock  created  for  that  Annuity,  which  was  at  the  rate  of  £52  2 7,  or  I .15  Ton  of  Iron  for  every 
£100  of  3 If  Cent  Stock,  whilst  in  1829  upwards  of  £2,500,000  out  of  the  Taxes  was  applied  to  the  purchase  of  Stock,  at  the  rate  of  £88  16,  or  4 .21  Tons  of  Iron 
for  £100  of  the  same  denomination  of  Stock.  If  however  this  seeming  disparity  of  Value  is  the  result  of  a corresponding  increase  and  facility  of  Pioduction  ; as 
far  as  the  question  of  internal  consumption  is  concerned,  it  may  be  regarded  as  a great  public  benefit : but,  as  regards  the  proportion  Exported,  a corresponding 
increase  of  production  of  some  kind  or  other  in  the  English  list  of  Imports,  ought  to  be  observable  ; none  however  appears,  nor  does  it  appear  that  the  consumers 
in  other  parts  of  the  World  have  been  benefited  by  the  increase  of  supply,  and  certainly  not  in  France,  or  the  United  States  of  North  America,  where  it  lias  only 
served  to  augment  the  Revenues  of  those  respective  Countries  : should  therefore  the  depreciation  of  Price  in  England,  be  the  result,  either  of  diminished  profits,  or 
diminished  reward  for  labor,  instead  of  increased  facility  of  production  ; it  proves,  that  British  profits  and  labor,  are  made  subservient  to  Taxation  abroad  as  well 
as  at  home,— a grievance,  which  Universal  Suffrage,  and  Vote  by  Ballot  will  not  remedy.
