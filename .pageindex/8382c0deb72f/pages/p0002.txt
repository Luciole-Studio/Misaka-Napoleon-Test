Paul’s Collection, the Nazarite Ritual and 2 Maccabees: James’ Masterstroke as Applied Game Theory in Jerusalem, 2024

This essay argues that Paul's collection and the sponsorship of the Nazarite ritual in Jerusalem concern an identical historical object. Some reflections on the relationship between the collection/ritual and doctrinal issues are presented. It is argued that (a) Paul was in a subordinate position relative to the Jerusalem leaders (especially James); and (b) that the collection Paul performed amongst his own converts served to undermine his message. Game theory is applied to show how the Jerusalem leaders used the collection to pin down Paul and neutralize the latter's insubordination. A probable parallel with a collection in 2 Maccabees is drawn. The leaders' (especially James') application of Paul's collection can then be appreciated as a brilliant masterstroke.

[The Athletic Metaphor in Paul's Writings](https://www.academia.edu/63129834/The_Athletic_Metaphor_in_Pauls_Writings)

[J. Michael Lester](https://vbc.academia.edu/JMichaelLester)

The athletic metaphor seems to transcend time and culture. In today’s culture, it’s not uncommon to hear “No pain, no gain,” or “Give it your best shot.” From the arena of boxing, people are challenged to not give up with the words, “Don’t throw in the towel.” From the realm of archery, the expression “He who aims at nothing hits it every time,” challenges this generation to have some concrete goals and pursue them. This paper aims to 1) Survey the development of the Pan-hellenic games, 2) State the discipline required of Grecian athletes, and 3) Show the usage of these metaphors in Pauline writings.
