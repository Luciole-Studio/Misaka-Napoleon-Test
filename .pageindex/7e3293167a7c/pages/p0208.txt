   A n d 1810 was the peak year of the English license trade.
T h e British government issued 18,356 licenses for illicit trade
with France and her allies in that single twelve-month period.*
It was also the year that disillusioned England with the license
trade. So m a n y of these licenses were issued to foreigners that
British shippers complained that the license trade was an ill-
founded scheme whereby fees for freighting British goods were
taken away from the British merchant marine.5 S o m e Britons
also complained of the power which the British government
gained over the merchant class by granting or withholding
licenses. Everyone bewailed the cultivation of forgery and
perjury which was part and parcel of the license trade.
