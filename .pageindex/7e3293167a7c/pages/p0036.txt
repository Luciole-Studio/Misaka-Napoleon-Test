   Young America, more than w e have ever realized, was eco­
nomically tied to Russia. In 1800, the average American
blacksmith used either Swedish or Russian iron if called on to
mak e anything finer or stronger than horseshoes or andirons;
and the American sailor, possibly the most important individ­
ual in our young economy, thought twice—and twice
again—before h e took any craft without Russian rigging,
cables, and sails beyond the harbor mouth. T o an appreciable
extent, the American economy survived and prospered because
it had access to the unending labor and rough skill of the
Russian muzhik.
