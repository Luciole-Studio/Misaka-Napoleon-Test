   All was to no avail. Russian imports of English tobacco fell
off sharply after 1700, and by the third quarter of the century
Russia was exporting considerable amounts of tobacco herself.
In fact, w h e n the American Revolution cut the supply of
American tobacco to West Europe, an appreciable amount of
the slack was taken up by increased importation of Russian
tobacco.12
   A s our colonial era progressed, our exports of tobacco to
Russia were supplemented by rice, indigo, tropical goods w e
picked up in the West Indies, and—incredibly—furs. In 1775,
Russia, one of the greatest fur producers in the world, imported
46,460 American beaver skins and 7,143 American otter skins
through St. Petersburg. A contemporary English writer said
that the motive for this strange Russian yen for American furs
arose simply from the fact that they were "far-fetched and dear
bought." 13
   In return for these goods, Russia sent us some of our most

In fact, this whole book will be something of a h y m n of praise
