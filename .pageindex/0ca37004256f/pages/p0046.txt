[87] Schroeder defines bandwagoning as “joining the stronger side for the sake of protection and payoffs.” Schroeder, “Historical Reality vs. Neo-realist Theory,” 117. Note that many IR scholars conceive of bandwagoning as aiming primarily for protection, not payoffs. Stephen Walt, for example, defines bandwagoning as “alignment with the source of danger.” Stephen M. Walt, Origins of Alliances (Ithaca: Cornell University Press, 1987), 17. Emphasizing positive payoffs is Randall L. Schweller, “Bandwagoning for Profit: Bringing the Revisionist State Back In.” International Security 19:1 (Summer 1994): 72-107. Hiding is related to but broader than the concept of buckpassing.
