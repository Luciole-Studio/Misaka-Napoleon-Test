In the Napoleonic period warfare ceased to be a matter for armies alone — 
it also became an affair of the people, or so runs the usual claim. In Spain, 
Portugal, Italy, Germany and Russia outraged peasants and townsfolk rose 
against the French armies and fell upon them without mercy. From these 
insurrections we get the modern word ‘guerrilla’, but did armed civilians 
really play an important a role in the struggle? In this collection of essays 
specialists on the Napoleonic epoch tease out the question, and arrive at 
some startling conclusions. 
Charles J. Esdaile lectures on the Revolutionary and Napoleonic Wars, 
modern Europe and the Spanish Civil War at the University of Liverpool, 
UK. His publications include The Spanish Army in the Peninsular Wars 
(1988), The Duke of Wellington and the Command of the Spanish Army 
(1990) and The Wars of Napoleon (1995).
