Their main objection was simply to the novelty of the proposal. There was nowhere in Europe any corporation for the insurance of ships; the business had always been carried on in the same way as at present, and that method was generally approved both at home and abroad. Seeing that "the insuring of Ships is of absolute Necessity for the carrying on of foreign Trade," the responsibility of recommending any innovation was greater than they cared to take. If any experiment was to be made in so important a matter it should be only on the advice of Parliament. For these reasons, they had come to the definite conclusion, "We cannot advise the erecting a Corporation for the insuring Ships and Goods at Sea."
