it  at  first  fairly  fresh  about  north-west,  but  the  Combined

and  west-north-west.  There  was  also  a  heavy  swell  rolling 
in  from  the  same  quarter  which  made  manoeuvring  still 
more  difficult,  and  the  result  was  that,  as  the  allies  began  to 
execute  the  new  movement,  the  confusion  increased  and  all 
semblance  of  order  disappeared.^ 

In  these  conditions  it  has  been  assumed  that  the  proper 
and  natural  thing  for  Nelson  to  do  was  to  restore  his  order 
of  sailing  and  bear  up  together,  or  at  least  to  form  the  line 
of  bearing  so  as  to  get  the  fleet  into  the  position  the 
Memorandum  depicted."  He  did  neither.  It  would  have 
been  contrary  to  the  established  practice.  Fleets  in  those 
days  rapidly  lost  order  and  cohesion  if  they  tried  to  move 
any  distance  in  line-abreast  or  line  of  bearing,  and  it
