The  Superb  was  a  very  powerful  ship  of  a  new  type  which  was  copied 
from  the  Pompee,  captured  at  Toulon.  With  her  carronades  she  threw  a 
broadside  of  1000  lbs.  The  Canopies  (a  prize  taken  in  1798)  had  a  broadside 
of  1092  lbs.  The  Victory's  original  armament  {i.e.  without  carronades)  gave 
a  broadside  of  1068  lbs.  The  Donegal  was  also  a  French  prize  of  1798,  even 
larger  than  the  Superb.  After  her  re-armament  with  carronades  she  was 
sometimes  classed  as  an  80-gun  ship.  The  Tigre,  which  brought  up  the  rear 
of  Nelson's  division,  was  also  a  heavy  French  prize  captured  in  1798,  and 
equal  in  force  to  the  Superb,  as  were  also  Bclleisle  and  Spurtiate.  The  rest 
were  small  74's.  The  unequal  distribution  of  ships  in  the  squadrons  shows 
that  the  divisional  and  not  the  squadroual  organisation  was  to  be  used.  In 
actual  sailing  Nelson  did  not  occupy  his  station,  but,  as  the  practice  was, 
led  the  starboard  line.  Under  June  5th  he  enters  in  his  Journal,  "  Directed 
Canopus  to  take  her  station  astern  of  the  Victory  and  the  Northumberland  to 
lead  the  lee  line  agreeable  to  the  order  of  battle  and  sailing  issued  to  the 
Fleet."  With  our  knowledge  of  what  happened  at  Trafalgar  it  may  well  be 
doubted  whether  Nelson  would  ever  have  got  back  into  his  place  if  the  enemy 
had  been  met  with.
