Apart  from  the  striking  concentration  of  powerful  units 
in  the  van  of  each  line  the  document  presents  other  re- 
markable features.  It  will  be  observed  that  the  organisa- 
tion is  quadruple  in  two  squadrons  and  four  divisions,  each 
with  its  flag-oflScer.  This  was  an  innovation,  but  whether 
Nelson's  or  not  cannot  yet  be  said  with  certainty.  The 
normal  organisation  since  the  reforms  of  Howe  and  Kem- 
penfelt  had  been  threefold,  that  is,  in  three  squadrons,  and 
for  a  fleet  of  this  size  each  squadron  would  be  in  two  divi- 
sions. It  had  become  usual  also  to  provide  for  a  dual 
formation  in  two  "  Grand  Divisions  "  by  splitting  the  centre 
squadron,  and  attaching  its  two  divisions  to  the  van  and 
rear  squadrons  respectively.  It  will  further  be  noted  that 
no  provision  is  made  for  the  Advance  Squadron  which 
the  Memorandum  contemplated,  as  had  been  done  in 
earlier  Mediterranean  orders/
