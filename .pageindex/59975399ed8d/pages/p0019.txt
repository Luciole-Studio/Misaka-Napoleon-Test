      Napoleonic France, Britain contrived to maintain a fleet in being in the
Mediterranean, supplies for which were largely drawn from the ports of the
Neapolitan kingdom, with which was included the kingdom of Sicily. In
1793 and 1798 Britain concluded treaties with Naples, entered into a secret
agreement with the government of Naples in 1803, and signed further
treaties with that government in Sicily in 1808, 1809, and 1812, all of which
conceded to Britain the use of the harbors of the two kingdoms and from
1806, by necessity, those which were situated in Sicily only. The British
preoccupation with Sicily, as indeed with nearby Malta, arose not only from
her concern to keep her naval squadrons provisioned, but also from her
realization of Napoleon’s concern to extend his rule beyond Italy to the
Levant. It requires no very deep grasp of strategy to see, from the briefest
glance at a map, Sicily’s importance to any naval power that sought to
dominate the Mediterranean. When in the spring of 1793 Pitt decided, once
France had declared war, to send a strong fleet to the Mediterranean, he
hoped that the Italian states would join in opposing the general threat the
French revolutionary government posed. Naples, which had the most to
offer so far as naval help was concerned, was hesitant to commit herself until
a British fleet had appeared, having been earlier forced by a French one into
declaring an unwilling neutrality. However, by the middle of July, a treaty of
alliance with Britain was signed, and ships, troops, and sailors were promised
in return for British naval protection. Neapolitan troops and ships cooper-
ated in the siege of Toulon and only a serious revolt in Naples, led by
sympathizers with the Jacobin cause, prevented serious help being given to
the British in their, assault on Corsica. However, by 1795 four Neapolitan
ships-of-the-line were successfully serving with the British fleet and, a year
later, a squadron of gunboats; and when Napoleon seized Leghorn, the main
supply base for Admiral Jervis, the ports of Naples gave vital assistance in
providing all that the admiral required.
