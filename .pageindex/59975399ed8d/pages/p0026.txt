   Stuart required no further urging. By the end of March he had crossed to
Sicily and installed two weak regiments in Messina citadel, to the obvious
delight of Nelson and Hamilton and of Ferdinand and his queen, who
ordered the governor and troops at Messina henceforth to take their orders
from Stuart. Stuart was able to dismiss the garrison of hated Neapolitan
troops and to supplement his British force by enlisting 2,000 Sicilian re-
cruits. In May, Colonel Graham was sent from Minorca to take over com-
mand of this force. He was even offered a seat on the council of the
governing body of Messina, but he had to decline because the duties pre-
scribed to him were purely military. Graham and the two British regiments
remained in Messina until December, by which time the danger had long
receded, and they were then sent to Malta to join Captain Ball and assist him
in the siege of Valletta.17
