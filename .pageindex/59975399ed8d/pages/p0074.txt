Bunbury found it incredible that at the moment when Sicily seemed to be
threatened with the greatest danger, Moore and so many excellent troops
should be removed to another theater, leaving behind to guard the island a
force of only 9,000 men. Russia was now an ally of France, and Marmont
had in the Adriatic area a disposable force of 40,000 that could be trans-
ported by French ships at Venice and by Russian warships at Corfu and
Trieste. And at Toulon and at Genoa there were French troops as well as the
ships to carry them, while their fleet could easily be joined by a Spanish one
from Cartagena. Ministers in London had taken a risk, one that was nearly to
lose them Sicily, and as soon as Castlereagh realized that the French had
taken over Corfu and that the threat to Sicily was real, he decided to reinforce
the latter.3?
   Moore’s replacement in Sicily was the same John Stuart who had been
there before, prior to the arrival of Henry Fox, the victor of Maida and friend
of the court. Perhaps to ingratiate himself with the ministers, he had told
them that he considered that Sicily could be held with a garrison of only
10,000, given sufficient naval support. Fox and Moore, on the other hand,
insisted it needed 26,000. Castlereagh decided to compromise, increased the
garrison to 16,000 by the early months of 1808 and, ignoring the possibility
of it having to be evacuated, ordered home the available transports.*°
