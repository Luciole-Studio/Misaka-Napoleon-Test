     8. Drummond had had the poorest opinion of Sicilian nobles as potential minis-
ters. He told Fox in April 1807 that he had made some attempt to get Prince
Belmonte included in the cabinet and went on, “But who is there in the country, with
the exception of Belmonte (of whom I could not think favourably, from the many
reports I have heard) who could have been brought into the Government? Pantelleria
is Our avowed enemy, Cassaro is governed by Brissac, General Acton is hated by the
whole country; Butera is shallow and without principles; Sambuca is too old; Trabea
is a buffoon; Tommasi is a man of low birth, with great knowledge is capable of
playing any game, and therefore, if he could be useful, he might be dangerous.”
Drummond to Fox, 28 April 1807, Bunbury, Military Transactions, 117-18 and n.
