      At last, feeling he must do something with an army of 16,000 lying idle,
and convincing himself that the Austrian reverses were not as severe as
French reports made out, he decided to capture the island of Ischia and to use
it as a base for further operations. This was a course that the court approved,
though Collingwood considered it a waste of effort.2© The expedition on
board included a large contingent of the court entourage, including Prince
Leopold, Circello, Tommasi, Saint Clair, Ascoli, and General Nunziante, and
they insisted on making landfalls at Policastro and Sapri en route in anticipa-
tion of picking up numerous recruits for the expedition. These landings
proved a complete farce and showed only too clearly that the local people
were not at all ready to welcome the Bourbons. By the time the convoy
arrived off Ischia, Stuart had lost all confidence in the successful outcome of
his enterprise and would probably have returned to Sicily at once had the
Ischia garrison not surrendered so easily.27
   Landing at Ischia on 25 June, Stuart was soon in control of the island and
of its neighbor Procida, but instead of assaulting Naples forthwith, he wasted
time in prolonged discussions about the nature of the new administration,
the court having sent along Prince Leopold, who suddenly proclaimed
himself viceroy. Murat had taken immediate steps to strengthen the defenses
of his capital, and Stuart realized too late that the moment for attacking it had
passed.?8
