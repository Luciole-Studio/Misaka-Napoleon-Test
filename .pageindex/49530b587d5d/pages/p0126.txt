Of the 128 captives, 96, that is, three quarters of them, found free-
dom, most of them by ransom; almost all the others died in captivity,
usually from illness. We know that because the purpose of their cap-
ture was obtaining ransom—torture and various forms of abuse were
almost non-existent. The few facts that we have tried to assemble
here, when they are reliable and complete, which is rare, can only
provide a rough idea of the situation. The restoration of corsair
activities at the end of the eighteenth century is shown in the increase
in the total number of slaves, as can be seen in the case of Algiers:7
