Poor captures, which were disappointing to the corsairs, were in fact
quite rare, 11% of the total; they were less numerous than the most
valuable captures, sixteen as against nineteen. In reality, the major-
ity were modest prizes, from ten to less than forty thousand francs.
    Dividing the ships captured by the Algerians and sailing under
the ﬁve most important ﬂags into four groups according to the value
of the captures, as we have done above, reveals considerable diﬀerences
from one ﬂag to another. With 59 ships out of 145 (40.7%), the
Kingdom of Naples provided the greatest number of victims for the
Algerians, but the value of the great majority of these captures was
low: 81.3% of the poor captures, at less than ten thousand francs,
were Neapolitan, as were 48.1% of the mediocre ones, at below
37,000 francs. On the other hand, the Neapolitans seldom ﬁgure
among the average captures, at 21.2%, and even less often in the
list of the valuable captures, with two ships, that is, 10.5% in this
category. The document unfortunately gives no data concerning the
type and tonnage of the captured ships, but the statement relating
to their cargo, though cursory, sheds some light on the question of
their value.
