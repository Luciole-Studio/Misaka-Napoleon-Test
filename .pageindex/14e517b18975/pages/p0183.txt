  It was only with the Peace,of Jassy that Selim was able to give
the reformers a free hand by dismissing Grand Vezir Koja Yusuf
Pasha (May 3, 1792) and replacing him with a puppet, the aged
and ailing Melek Mehmed Pasha, who was over eighty years of
age and entirely without power of his own. For all practical pur¬
poses, real authority was now in the hands of the reformers, some
holding official positions and some not, who met regularly in an
unofficial reform council as well as in the regular Imperial Coun¬
cil to debate, enact, and execute their reform decrees. Leading
this group were those who had risen with the Sultan and sub¬
mitted the reform memorials to him during the course of the war:
Chelebi Mustafa Reshid Efendi, lieutenant of the Grand Vezir
and originator of the Nizam-i Jedid army and treasury; Kuchiik
Hiiseyin Pasha, whose power rested not only on his close friend¬
ship with the Sultan and successes with the fleet, but also on his
marriage with Abd ul-Hamid I’s daughter, Esma Sultan; Yusuf
Agha, Director of the Mint, particularly powerful because of his
position as lieutenant of Selim’s mother, the Valide Sultan;
Tataijik Abdullah Efendi, who had a great deal of influence not
only as a result of the length and breadth of his reform proposals
but also because of his position as the only member of the Ulema
in the group, and thus Selim’s only hope of reconciling the re¬
forms with them; and three somewhat less influential men who
alternated as Reis ul-Kuttab to handle foreign relations, Ingiliz
Mahmud Raif Efendi, Mehmed Rashid Efendi, and Ebubekir
Ratib Efendi.^
  Almost as soon as the reformers came to power, began to enact
their decrees, and met some opposition, their coalition broke up.
Each man strove to divert popular displeasure onto his colleagues
in order to advance his own position at their expense. Less than
two months after the Peace of Jassy was signed, in April 1792, the
rivalry began when Yusuf Agha and the Valide Sultan got Selim
to take Kuchiik Hiiseyin out of the palace, appoint him as Grand
Admiral, and send him on a long naval expedition to the Mediter-
