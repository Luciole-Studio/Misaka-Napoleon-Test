    33. Divan-i Humayun Katibi. Cevdet^, VI, 9, gives this name as
Sam‘i Efendi, but the manuscript reports themselves invariably give
Safi, and a person of this name was Beylikci of the Imperial Council at
this time (SO, III, 236), while no record can be found of a Sam'i Efendi
in Ottoman service at this or any other time.
    34. D, IV, 642.
    35. D’Ohsson served as Dragoman to the Swedish embassy in Istan¬
bul for many years, and then as Swedish minister to the Porte between
1796 and 1799. His sympathy with the French Revolution and assistance
to its agents in Turkey eventually led to his dismissal in disgrace, and
he spent the remaining years of his life petitioning various governments
for money to maintain him while he compiled his massive description of
the Ottoman Empire, which was eventually published as Tableau gen¬
eral de I’Empire Othoman, 2nd ed., 7 vols. (Paris, 1788-1824). Although
this work was written during Selim’s reign, it has very little to say about
his reforms directly. His role is discussed in FO 78/15, no. 7 (10 April
1797).
