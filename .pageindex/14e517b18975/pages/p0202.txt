in foreign languages and techniques for military purposes were
able to penetrate the great store of European literature and to
perceive more clearly the whole range of European life and
thought. The effectiveness of the new army, when contrasted
with the increasing inefficiency and anarchy of the Janissaries
and Spahis, provided a fitting lesson for all who could see. Al¬
though when the crisis came Selim betrayed the reformers to
their enemies in order to save himself, enough of them survived
and benefited from experience to nourish the seed and provide a
cadre of men able and willing to restore the reforms and move
beyond them as soon as the political situation made this possible
in the reign of Mahmud II.
