          Lazarev informed Galib Efendi that the Czar had added condi¬
          tions concerning the Serbs and that no further evacuation could
          take place without new negotiations; Galib therefore declared
          the armistice at an end and returned to Istanbul. On October 21,
          General Prosorovich arrived at Bucharest to take command of
          the Russian army, and both sides began preparations for a spring
          campaign.^^ The war went on, albeit somewhat sporadically, and
          was actually ended only five years later by the Treaty of Bucha¬
          rest. The immediate Ottoman reaction in Istanbul was renewal
          of the earlier anti-French feeling; once again, it was felt that
          Bonaparte had betrayed the Sultan at Tilsit, that Bonaparte
          never intended that the Russians evacuate their positions, and
          that Sebastiani had intentionally been trying to deceive the
          Sultan. Mehmed Efendi tried to save himself from the conse¬
          quences of failure by complaining about Sebastiani’s deception to
          the Imperial Council and to Bonaparte, asking for his immediate
          recall. At the same time, he called Paget to Istanbul and began
          secret negotiations to secure British evacuation from Egypt.
