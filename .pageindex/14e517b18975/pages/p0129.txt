  As soon as Ali returned from Vidin in September, he impris¬
oned the French agents sent to him and took Vonitza, Butrinto
(September 28) and Preveza (October 13), most of whose Greek
population fled in terror to Corfu and Santa Maura. Ali finally
was in a position where he could successfully outflank and block¬
ade the Suliotes and people of Parga and thus fully establish his
control of the Albanian coast for the first time. At the same time,
he moved to satisfy his long-standing ambition to add the Ionian
Islands to his state. He immediately began to prepare an expedi¬
tionary force for this purpose and requested the Sultan’s permis¬
sion to accomplish this in his behalf.^^
  Much to Ali’s chagrin, the combined Ottoman-Russian fleet
arrived just in time to forestall him. After leaving the Dar¬
danelles on October 1, the fleet had first gone to Hydra, where joint
proclamations were sent to the people of the islands asking for
their cooperation against the French and promising to establish
any form of government they wanted once peace was achieved.^®
French rule in the islands since 1797 actually had been extremely
beneficial to the inhabitants. The native aristocracy, which had
ruled dictatorially under the Venetians, had been undermined by
reforms abolishing feudalism, freeing the peasants from forced
labor, reorganizing justice, and extending education. However,
increasingly heavy taxation to support the French garrison and
the arrogant and forced manner by which the reforms were intro¬
duced alienated not only the aristocrats and the church, who
