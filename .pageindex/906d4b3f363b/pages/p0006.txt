In addition, Napoleon’s establishing of the French fiscal model in the four corners of Europe marked the beginning of a modern understanding of public finance. But this financial rigour came at a price. In France and in the annexed territories, the political price Napoleon paid for re-introducing indirect contributions (tax on alcoholic beverages, tobacco and salt) was as high as that for conscription. More importantly, his desire to make war pay for itself was to scar diplomatic relations in Europe for generations; as evidenced by the grudge that Prussia bore France until 1870 and beyond.
