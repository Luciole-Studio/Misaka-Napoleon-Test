The election of 1810 II presented a fuzzy picture, at best. 
Certainly some candidates, especially those striving to oust in- 
cumbents, promised that they would fight for a forthright national 
policy. Certainly a few results reflected growing discontent with 
the cowardice shown by Congress since 1808. Still, few newspapers 
claimed at the time that thenew House of Representatives differed 
much from its predecessor. Most congressmen came to Washington 
in the fall of 1811 unpledged, probably undecided. Events be- 
tween the elections and the congressional vote for war, an interval 
sometimes as lon^ as two years, tipped the balance in favor of 
armed resistance. \
