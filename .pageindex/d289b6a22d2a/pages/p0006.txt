     As with the Landwehr recruiting, the methods for obtaining funds for the Berliner Landwehr were not well-
organised. On 15 April 1813, ‘an official notice about the establishment of the Landwehr fund44’ was published in
the Berlinische Nachrichten. According to this order, households in Berlin paid 1 groschen per 1 thaler of rent for the
Landwehr fund as a rent tax; however, this tax was insufficient. As indicated in the financial report of the Berliner
Landwehr fund published in the newspaper, the fund paid out a total of 127,374 thalers by July 1813, while the tax
covered only 40,570 thalers. Furthermore, about 10% of the tax was unpaid45. The city council in Berlin borrowed
10,000 thalers, but the budget for the Berliner Landwehr was lacking46. So, they attempted to cover the lack of
recruiting and funds through contributions. The next chapter examines the contributions to the Berliner Landwehr.

4. Contributions in the Berlinische Nachrichten during the Wars of
Liberation

      Before the Wars of Liberation, some contribution notices were published in the Berlinische Nachrichten. The
institutions that received the donations assigned a number to each contribution for one purpose and posted a notice
with the name (or initials) and profession of the contributor in the local newspaper. Most of the contributors were
of the private wealthy class47. The contribution notice was a declaration of the contributor’s merits and an adver-
tisement for more contributions. The form of these notices in the newspapers did not change during the Wars of
Liberation.
      From the beginning of the war in 1813, the contributions were primarily provided instead to the war effort and
the military. Such contributions amounted to approximately 6.5 million thalers in Prussia48. The Prussian govern-
ment and the King praised any contributions to the war effort as a patriotic action49. Historical researchers have
