Until now, the French flanking forces, some operating more than a hundred
miles away from the main army group, as well as Reynier's Saxons and
Dombrowski's column, operating more or less independently, have received
only passing mention in this narrative. However, as the main army group
marched beyond Smolensk and toward Moscow, the operations of these
forces steadily assumed greater importance, as the flanking forces the Russians
had deployed gradually came into action and eventually closed in until they
began to exercise a direct effect upon the events in the center and the main
army's line of communications.
