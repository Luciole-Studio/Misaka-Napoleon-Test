Napoleon had been reviewing Ney's troops and had just returned from
Bogorodsk when he was told of the action at Vinkovo, mere hours after it
had taken place. He at once issued orders for the retreat. In the afternoon
of the same day, the 18th, the greatest part of the army, I and IV Corps,
was sent to bivouac in front of the city, along the road to Kaluga. The rest,
the Guard and Ney's III Corps, remained in Moscow.
       But Napoleon had waited too long. Now, just as he finally made up his
mind to move, events on his flanks took a turn that brought into question
whether he would still find the road to the rear open.
