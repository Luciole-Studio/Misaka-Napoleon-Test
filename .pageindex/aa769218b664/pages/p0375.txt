                           WITTGENSTEIN
The loss of the second battle of Polotsk made it impossible for the French
to maintain the Dvina line. After St. Cyr had been put out of action, three
generals each went their own way. Going behind the Ula, Legrand took his
division to Beszenkoviczi; Wrede took what was left of Bavarian VI Corps
to Glubkoye; while the remainder of 11 Corps, Cuirassier Division Doumerc,
and Light Cavalry Brigade Castex, as well as the two remaining divisions,
Maison (ex-Verdier) and Merle, the latter in pro forma command, went to
Czasniki.
    When Victor heard of these events, he marched toward the Ula at once,
sending Daendels' division to Beszenkoviczi while he himself went to Czas-
niki.^With Victor on the scene, there was no longer any question about
who was in command. When the two corps united between October 29 and
31, Victor had 36,000 men at his disposal, including 4000 cavalry. Of these,
14,000 were from II Corps and 22,000 from IX Corps, slightly weakened by
detachments.
    After repairing the bridges at Polotsk on the 23rd, Wittgenstein crossed
the Dvina on the 24th. Since he had failed to push St. Cyr away from the
French line of retreat, as called for in the tsar's operations plan, but had,
instead, pushed him toward it, he decided to compensate for this by attacking
