       For the broods of sows, especially troublesome to estimate since a sow can have up to three litters per year of ten or more piglets, Trow-Smith's evidence leads him to believe that two litters every twelve
       to fourteen months, each of five to six piglets, was normal in seventeenth-century England (ibid., 252), again somewhat higher than my estimate. Local practices are the major determining factor, however,
       and Spanish rates cannot be expected to duplicate those of England. Data on birth rates and life expectancy of animals are drawn from the corresponding entries in the Enciclopedia universal ilustrada
       europeo-americana (Barcelona and Bilbao, Espasa-Calpe, 1908–30), which I use as most likely to reflect Spanish conditions.
