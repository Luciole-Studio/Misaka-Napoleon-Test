       APPENDIX G—
       THE CASA EXCUSADA
       In 1761 the crown issued instructions on the administration of the casa excusada. The administrators were to select the largest tither in
       each parish (including parishes annexed to other churches), whether owner or tenant, layman or religious institution, except the Order of
       San Juan. The casa excusada had to pay its tithes to the administrator and no one else, but at the time and according to the custom of
       local tithes. The administrator farmed the collection from the crown, putting up a fianza, or bond, to insure a proper rendering of
       accounts. [1] The instruction makes clear that prior to this date "primeras casas dezmeras" were not separated from the common body of
       tithers. Protests raised by parish priests in 1761 and 1762 that the reform would hurt their income are further evidence that before this
       date the tithes of the first tither had gone into the general fund, the partible. [2] In 1764 the crown farmed the collection of the excusado
       to the Five Great Guilds of Madrid, but after 1778 bishops began to take over the contracts for their dioceses, until by 1789 very little
       was left to the Five Guilds. [3] After 1793 the Five Great Guilds again administered it. [4] The excusado was still being collected separately
       from the other tithes in 1800 and 1801.[5]

       [1] Instrucción, 24 Jan. 1761, AHN, Hac., libro 8022, no. 1679.

       [2] RD, 16 July 1761, Nov. rec. II, xii, 5, and RD, 14 Jan. 1762, ibid., ley 6.

       [3] Capella and Matilla, Cinco Gremios Mayores, 191–95.
