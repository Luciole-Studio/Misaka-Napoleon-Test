Rural Change and Royal Finances in Spain




                                                                                   7.
                                           The Preparation of the Map of Tomás López. About 1766 the royal geographer
                                          Tomás López sent a form letter to the parish priests throughout Spain requesting
                                       information on the roads passing through their towns, the rivers or bridges the roads
                                     crossed, the other places to which the roads led, and the direction and distance of these
                                        places from their own town. (A copy of his letter can be found in Biblioteca Nacional,
                                   Sección de Manuscritos, MS. 7307, f. 279.) A number of the responses to his request included
                                    rough maps. This is the map sent in from the town of Porcuna in Jaén. It shows the town's
                                    relationship to surrounding towns, and the roads and rivers of the region. Lopera is located
                                         north of Porcuna, and the Guadalquivir River appears just below the Sierra Morena
                                           at the  top, including the important bridge at Andújar. (Ibid., MS. 7301, f. 350)




                                                                                 8.
                                  The Map of Jaén of Tomás López, 1787. On the basis of the information Tomás López received,
                                he created the first complete set of maps of the provinces of Spain, produced in large, hand-drawn
                                volumes, copies of which are now in the Biblioteca Nacional and the Real Academia de la Historia.
