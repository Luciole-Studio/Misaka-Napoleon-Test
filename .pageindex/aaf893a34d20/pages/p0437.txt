       APPENDIX H—
       RELATIVE PRICES OF CROPS, 1753–99
       In Table 7.19 the percent of the total value of the harvest in La Mata of each crop at different dates is based on a constant price ratio of
       the crops. The only ratio whose change would significantly modify Table 7.19 is that of garbanzos to wheat. I have no information on this
       ratio in Salamanca after 1753 until the mid-nineteenth century. Figures given by Hamilton for New Castile show the following evolution of
       prices: [1]


                             1752–56            1770–74         1792–1800


            Wheat                100               124               200


          Garbanzos              100                82               121

              (The period 1792–1800 refers to the years 1792, 1793, 1795, 1798, and 1800, five years for which data for both crops are given.)
             These data indicate a decline in the ratio garbanzos:wheat. Yet by the mid-nineteenth century the reverse was true. In Toledo in
       1845–54, the price ratio garbanzos:wheat (by volume) was 2.45 : 1.[2] In Salamanca in 1753 it was 1.79 : 1 (Table 7.4). According to
       Cabo Alonso, the quintal of wheat in the mid-nineteenth century in La Armuña was worth about 74 reales, the quintal of garbanzos about
       288;[3] this is a ratio of 3.9 : 1 by weight or about 3.5 : 1 by volume. [4] These figures suggest that the price of garbanzos may have
       been rising faster than the price of wheat in Salamanca in the second half of the eighteenth century (despite Hamilton's data). If so, the
       strategy of the farmers of La Mata of switching to garbanzos would have increased the value of their harvest. But this hypothesis remains
       in the realm of conjecture.
