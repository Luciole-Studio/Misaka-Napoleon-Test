       APPENDIX J—
       MODIFICATIONS IN PARTIBLE TITHES AFTER 1796
       In 1796 a papal breve and royal cédula revised the regulations on lands exempt from tithes (tierras horras ), requiring them henceforth
       to contribute to the partible tithes. [1] The breve states that in order better to support parish priests, church fabrics, and the poor, all
       privileges of exemption from payment of tithes are abolished, whatever endowment or religious institution may possess them. How it
       applied in a particular case is not clear. A royal resolution of 5 December 1796 appears to say that it did not abolish the right of religious
       communities and other endowments to collect tithes on their lands; it only abolished exemptions from paying any tithes. [2] (If the king
       were only interested in obtaining his tercias reales, then he did not care who received the rest of the tithes, but this instruction violates
       the purpose of helping priests and poor.) Understood this way, it would appear not to apply to La Mata, since the renteros of exempt
       lands did pay tithes (or a fixed payment in lieu of tithes) to their owners. Yet in 1799 (for the first time) the tithe register has a separate
       list of payments of "new tithes as ordered by the breve of His Holiness" ("nuevos diezmos como está mandando por el breve de su
       Santidad"). The two individuals who we know from monastic account books rented tierras horras, however, do not figure among those
       who paid nuevos diezmos. [3] Furthermore the monastery Nuestra Señora del Jesús negotiated a new lease in 1803 that still specified
       payment of "los horros correspondientes."
