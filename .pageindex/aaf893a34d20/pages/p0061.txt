       the total.
             Thus the financial advisers of Fernando VII believed that a greater proportion of property of the church had been sold than we
       estimate. They were referring to all Spain, not just Castile; nevertheless, I believe our figure to be closer to the truth, although 15
       percent may be too low. One can be reasonably sure that a sixth of all ecclesiastical property was disentailed. In most provinces of
       Andalusia it was a fifth or more, and, if the figures of the catastro are correct, it was almost a third in Murcia. One must conclude that
       the disentail of Carlos IV was an event of major significance in the history of the Spanish church and church-state relations.
