This  Island  was  discovered  by  Sebastian  Cabot  in  1497,  but  no 
settlement  was  made  upon  it  by  the  English,  and  it  was  for  some  time 
occupied  by  the  French  as  a  fishing  station.  It  was  first  taken  into 
their  possession  by  the  English  in  1758,  and  has  since  remained  subject 
to  this  country.

16,000;  in  1827  it  consisted  of  23,473  (12,211  males,  and  11,262 
females).  In  1841  a  census  was  taken,  according  to  which  the  inhabit-

The  progress  of  this  island  in  improvement  has  been  checked  by  an 
extraordinary  proceeding  of  the  English  Government  which,  in  1767 
granted  very  nearly  the  whole  surface  by  a  gratuitous  kind  of  lottery,  the 
holders  of  the  tickets  to  wliich  benefits  were  attached  being  bound  to 
pay  a  few  sliillings  per  annum  for  each  100  acres,  and  to  settle  tlieir 
lands  in  the  proportion  of  one  settler  for  every  200  acres  within  ten 
years  from  the  date  of  the  grant.  These  conditions  have  been  mainly 
evaded ;  the  grantees  were  for  the  most  part  permanently  absent  from 
the  island,  and  settlers  have  been  unwilling  to  embark  their  capital  and 
industry  in  the  improvement  of  property  which  they  could  not  make 
their  own,  while  on  the  neighbouring  continent  there  was  an  abundance 
of  land  to  be  had  in  fee  simple  and  on  easy  terras. 

There  are  comparatively  but  few  immigrants  now  resident  on  the 
island.  Of  the  47,033  persons  hving  there  in  1841,  there  were  31,561, 
or  about  two-thirds,  who  were  born  in  the  colony,  and  who  for  the  most 
part  were  descended  from  Scotchmen.
