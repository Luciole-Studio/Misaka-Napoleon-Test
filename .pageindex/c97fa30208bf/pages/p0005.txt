A nationwide selective examination was introduced for the recruitment of teachers, and still exists today. The foundation of the ‘Grandes Écoles’ had begun in the mid-eighteenth century, with the École des ponts et chaussées in 1747 followed by the École des mines in 1783. In 1794 the Revolutionaries established the École polytechnique, the Conservatoire national des arts et métiers and the École Normale; and the Restoration introduced the Ecole Normale supérieure de commerce (1819), among others. The effect on the general population was minimal. Literacy continued to improve during the period, but the trend witnessed no radical change. Men and North-eastern France continued to be considerably more literate than the rest of France.
