Over the whole after-war period, BoE directors had insisted that an effective resumption
was only possible if the government paid back a substantial amount of debt to the Bank.
The legal framework governing the definite resumption of specie payments - Peel’s Act,
enacted 2 July 1819 - imposed the repayment of £10 million of government short-term
debt and also made it illegal for the BoE to lend money to the government for more
than 3 months without approval of Parliament. This paved the way for resumption,
undertaken by 1 May, 1821.
