HAMBURGH,  BREMEN,  AND  NORTHERN  PORTS  OF  GERMANY. 

The  commerce  of  the  United  States,  with  Hamburgh,  and  Bremen, 
during  the  wars  in  Europe,  and  until  these  cities  were  occupied  by  the 
French  armies,  and  became  a  prey  to  French  rapacity,  was  extensive, 
particularly  with  the  former,  in  colonial  produce.  The  imperial  city 
of  Hamburgh  has  long  been  the  great  depot  of  the  extensive  com- 
merce of  Germany  and  the  north  of  Europe.  By  means  of  the  rivers 
Elbe  and  Weser,  and  the  canals,  connected  with  them,  the  manufac- 
tures of  Germany,  are  brought  to  Hamburgh ;  and  from  thence  ex- 
ported to  the  United  States,  and  other  parts  of  the  world.    Through
