The  ralue  of  manufactured  articles,  imported  into  the  United  States, 
from  Great  Britain,  has  far  exceeded  that  of  the  same  articles,  from 
all  other  countries ;  and  it  is  not  a  little  interesting,  to  the  American 
Economist,  as  well  as  Statesman,  to  ascertain  the  extent  of  this 
trade ;  and  particularly,  in  the  manufactures  of  wool,  cotton,  flax, 
silk,  and  iron,  since  1814. 

The  British  accounts,  in  connection  with  those  of  the  United 
States,  will  enable  us  to  do  this ;  and  will,  likewise,  shew  the  extent 
of  the  trade  of  this  country,  with  Great  Britain,  in  comparison  with 
that  enjoyed  by  other  nations.

XII,  annexed  to  this  chapter.
