     The area where cholera occurred (and still occurs) endemically is north-
eastern India, more precisely the river basins of the Ganges and Brahmaputra
as well as the historic province of Bengal situated between them. Since the
first confirmed outbreak of cholera outside that region in 1817 and its spread
around south-east Asia and northern Africa, many chronological divisions of the
pandemic have been made. Regarding the 19th century, most scholars distinguish
five pandemics, although they would not always agree on their beginning and
ending dates.1 This article shall focus on one year only, 1831, from the period
of the second pandemic which I date to the years 1826–1837.
