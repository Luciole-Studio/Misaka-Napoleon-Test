

[Siege of Sevastopol](https://cdn.britannica.com/45/126245-004-143CC9EF/forces-British-Russia-Sevastopol-lithograph-1855.jpg)British forces firing upon Sevastopol, Russia; lithograph, 1855.(more)

On October 23, 1853, Turkish forces attacked the advanced Russian troops in the Danubian principalities; on November 1, Russia declared war on Turkey. France and Britain declared war on Russia on March 27, 1854. The courage displayed in the [defense of Sevastopol](https://www.britannica.com/event/Siege-of-Sevastopol) proved useless, as the whole fabric of Russian [bureaucratic](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/bureaucratic) and autocratic government appeared incapable of competing with European technique. Corruption and lack of communication, feeble development of industry, and financial deficiency deprived the valiant soldiers of the most necessary means of defense. Nicholas died in [St. Petersburg](https://www.britannica.com/place/St-Petersburg-Russia) on March 2 (February 18, Old Style), 1855, feeling that all his system was doomed to destruction. A wholesale change of regime was indicated to his son and successor, [Alexander II](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Alexander-II-emperor-of-Russia).
