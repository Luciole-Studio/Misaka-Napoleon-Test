The British navy never experienced the desperate food shortages of the kind that so often forced French squadrons to return home. 34 In 1746 the French launched an expedition to North America that was beset with victualling disasters. The commander, D'Enville was forced to take out all the supplies he needed with him, including sufficient victuals for the return voyage. On arriving in Santa Maria on the other side of the Atlantic, provisions on several vessels were already starting to diminish. Remarkable amounts of victuals were condemned, while 40% of the fleets' naval strength failed to reach Chibouctou. Much of this was down to victualling failure: on Le Mars half the crew perished from bad food, while on board Le Northumberland more than 50 men were dying daily at one point. Storeships were not organised for the 380,400 supplementary rations being prepared at Rochefort.
