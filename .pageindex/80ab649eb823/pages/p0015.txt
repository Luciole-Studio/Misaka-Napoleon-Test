xiv   Notes on Contributors


Annie Jourdan is a research fellow in European studies at the University
of Amsterdam. She has been a visiting professor at the University of Sussex
and at Florida State University. She has published several books on the
French and Dutch Revolutions and on the Napoleonic era, including La
Revolution, une exception française? (2004), La Révolution Batave entra la France
et L’Amérique (2008), L’empire de Napoléon (2000), Napoléon, Héros, Imperator,
Mécène (1998) and Les monuments de la Révolution. Une histoire de représenta-
tion (1997).
Silvia Marzagalli is Professor of Early Modern History at the University of
Nice, Director of the Centre de la Méditerranée Moderne et Contemporaine,
and senior member of the Institut Universitaire de France. Her publications
include Bordeaux et les États-Unis, 1776–1815: politique et stratégies négociantes
dans la genèse d’un réseau commercial (2014) and ‘Les boulevards de la fraude’.
Le négoce maritime et le Blocus continental, 1806–1813. Bordeaux, Hambourg,
Livourne (1999). She has also co-edited Rough Waters: The United States
Involvement in the Mediterranean, 18th–19th c. (2010), Atlas de la Révolution
française. Circulations des hommes et des idées, 1770–1804 (2010), Révolution
Consulat et Empire (2009) and Guerre et économie dans l’espace atlantique du
XVIe au XXe siècle (2006).
Pierrick Pourchasse is a senior lecturer and research fellow in the
Department of History at the University of Bretagne Occidentale in Brest.
His research deals with economic relations between France and Northern
Europe in the eighteenth century. He is the author of Le commerce du Nord.
Les échanges entre la France et l’Europe septentrionale au XVIIIe siècle (2006)
and has published in Histoire et Sociétés Rurales, Histoire, Économie et Société
and International Journal of Maritime History. He is one of the coordinators of
‘Navigocorpus’, a project which aims to create and publish important data
on merchant shipping between 1650 and 1850.
Michael Rowe is Senior Lecturer in Modern European History at King’s
College London. His book From Reich to State: The Rhineland in the
Revolutionary Age, 1780–1830 (2003) was joint winner of the Gladstone Prize
of the Royal Historical Society. His other publications include Collaboration
and Resistance in Napoleonic Europe: State-Formation in an Age of Upheaval,
c.1800–1815 (2003), ‘Napoleon and the “Modernization” of Germany’, in
Napoleon and His Empire: Europe, 1804–1814 (2006), ‘Napoleon’s France:
A Forerunner of Europe’s Twentieth-Century Dictatorships?’, in Rethinking
History, Dictatorship and War: New Approaches and Interpretations (2009), ‘Die
Sichtbarkeit der Macht: Symbolische Repraesentation von Herrschaft im
napoleonischen Kaiserreich’, Historische Zeitschrift (2012) and ‘The French
Revolution, Napoleon, and Nationalism in Europe’, in The Oxford Handbook
of the History of Nationalism (2013).
