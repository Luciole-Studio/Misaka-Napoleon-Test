Bård Frydenlund is a research fellow in history at the University of Oslo.
His publications include a historical biography on Norway’s first prime min-
ister Peder Anker (1749–1824): Stormannen Peder Anker. En biografi (2009).
He also published ‘Political Practices among Merchants in Denmark and
Norway in the Period of Absolutism’, in Scandinavia in the Age of Revolutions:
Nordic Political Cultures, 1740–1820 (2011), and ‘The Value of Friendship in
Trade and Political Networks: An Interactive View on the Late 18th Century
Urban Elite of Christiania’, in Friendship and Social Networks in Scandinavia
c. 1000–1800 (2013). He has edited several anthologies on Nordic history
from the eighteenth century and the Napoleonic era.
Alexander Grab is Adelaide and Alan Bird Professor of History at the
University of Maine. His book, Napoleon and the Transformation of Europe
(2003), won the first prize of the International Napoleonic Society in 2004.
His other publications comprise articles on taxation, education and con-
scription in Napoleonic Italy, including ‘Conscription and Desertion in
Napoleonic Europe’, in Wiener Zeitschrift zur Geschichter der Neuzeit (2008)
and ‘Public Education in Napoleonic Italy’, in Napoleon and His Empire,
1804–1814 (2007).
Hilde Greefs is Senior Lecturer in Modern Social History at the University
of Antwerp and is affiliated with the Centre for Urban History through her
research activities on networks, international trade and migration. Her pub-
lications on the Napoleonic era include ‘Un rêve abruptement interrompu.
Illusions et désillusions dans le milieu d’affaires sous le régime napoléonien
et les stratégies d’une “nouvelle” élite du commerce international à Anvers’,
in L’Empire napoléonien. Une expérience européenne? (2014) and ‘Dromen van
een nieuwe start. Napoleon en de maritieme handel’, in Bonaparte aan de
Schelde. Antwerpen in een Franse stroomversnelling (2013).
Johan Joor is an independent scholar and an honorary fellow of the
International Institute of Social History at Amsterdam. His research on the
Dutch Napoleonic experience has been published in French, Russian and
English as well as Dutch. His publications on the Napoleonic era include:
‘Resistance against Napoleon in the Kingdom of Holland’, in The Napoleonic
Empire and the New European Political Culture (2012), ‘Le système continental
et sa signification pour le Royaume de Hollande’, in Louis Bonaparte. Roi
de Hollande (2010), ‘A Very Rebellious Disposition: Dutch Experience and
Popular Protest under the Napoleonic Regime (1806–1813)’, in Soldiers,
Citizens and Civilians: Experiences and Perceptions of the Revolutionary and
Napoleonic Wars, 1790–1820 (2009) and De Adelaar en het Lam. Onrust,
opruiing en onwilligheid in Nederland ten tijde van het Koninkrijk Holland en de
Inlijving bij het Franse Keizerrijk (1806–1813) (2000).
