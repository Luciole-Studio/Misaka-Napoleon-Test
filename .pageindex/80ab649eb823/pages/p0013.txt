Notes on Contributors

Katherine B. Aaslestad is Professor of History at West Virginia University.
She is the author of Place and Politics: Local Identity, Civic Culture, and German
Nationalism in North Germany during the Revolutionary Era (2005). She has
co-edited special issues on war and gender in Central European History and
European History Quarterly. She has published more than a dozen articles and
book chapters on republican political culture in the Hanseatic cities, gender
and consumption, and the Napoleonic Wars in northern Europe. She is cur-
rently working on a book project, After the Wars: German Central Europe after
Napoleonic Conquest, 1815–1840.

Anita Čerpinska is a researcher at the Institute of Latvian History at
the University of Latvia. Her publications include ‘Als 1812 die Rigaer
Vorstädte brannten. Ein Rekonstruktionsversuch’, Forschungen zur Baltischen
Geschichte (2010), ‘Образ врага в публицистике Гарлиба Меркеля периода
войны 1812 г’ (“The Image of the Enemy in Publicism of Garlieb Merkel
during the War of 1812”), in Французский Ежегодник (2012), лет
Отечественной войны 1812 года (Annuaire d’Études Françaises) (2012), ред.
Серж Абердам и др. (2012).
Geoffrey Ellis is an Emeritus Fellow at Oxford University, where he was
Lecturer in French history and an Official Fellow and Tutor in Modern
History at Hertford College until 2004. His research focuses on French
history, and he is the author of Napoleon’s Continental Blockade: The Case
of Alsace (1981), The Napoleonic Empire (1991; revised 2003) and Napoleon
(1997/2000). He has also published several articles in collected volumes
and conference papers on Marxist interpretations of the French Revolution,
Napoleonic social elites, the revolution of 1848 in France, religion and
Napoleon and the nature of Napoleonic imperialism. He is currently work-
ing on an economic history of the French Revolution, 1787–99.

Alan Forrest is Emeritus Professor of Modern History at the University of
York and has also taught as a visiting professor at Institut d’Etudes Avancées,
Nantes. He has published widely on French Revolutionary and Napoleonic
history, on military experience in the Napoleonic armies and more generally
on the history and memory of the war. His books include: Conscripts and
Deserters: The Army and French Society during the Revolution and Empire (1989),
The Revolution in Provincial France: Aquitaine, 1789–1799 (1996), Napoleon’s
Men: The Soldiers of the Revolution and Empire (2002), Paris and the Provinces
and the French Revolution (2004), The Legacy of the French Revolutionary Wars:
The Nation in Arms in French Republican Memory (2009) and Napoleon: Life,
