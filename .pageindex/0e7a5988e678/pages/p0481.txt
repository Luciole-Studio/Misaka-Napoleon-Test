PORTUGUESE  DISCOVERIES  AND  CONQUESTS  IN  THE  EAST  INDIES. 

The  Portuguese  were  once  the  principal  navigators,  and  were  the  first  Europeans  who  explored  the 
Coast  of  Africa  to  the  southward,  and  visited  the  East  Indies  by  the  way  of  the  Cape  of  Good  Hope. 

1410,  Previous  to  this  period  the  Canary  Islands  were  discovered,  and  the  extent  of  the  S.  W.  coast 
of  Africa  known  to  the  Portuguese  was  Cape  Non.  In  this  year  they  fitted  out  ships  for  discovery,  ad- 
mitting  Spaniards  and  others  who  were  skilled  in  navigation,  into  their  service  for  that  purpose.  These 
vessels  proceeded  as  far  as  Cape  Bojadore,  but  did  not  double  that  promontory. 

1418.  The  Island  of  Porto  Sancto  was  discovered  by  Tristan  Vas,  on  the  Feast  of  All  Saints. 

1419.  The  island  of  Madeira  was  first  visited  by  the  Portuguese,  when  it  was  ascertained  that  it 
had  been  visited  by  an  Englishman  named  Macham,  about  the  year  1844.
