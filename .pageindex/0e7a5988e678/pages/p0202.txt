The  measurement  is  what  the  Dutch  call  Rynlandsch,  and  is  a trifle  larger  than  the  English; 
consequently  the  above  dimensions,  when  the  English  rule  is  applied,  should  be  full  measure. 

The  pipe  staves  of  Teak  timber,  although  not  used  for  making  casks,  as  the  Teak  tastes  the  wine, 
are  sometimes  used  for  spirits.  They  are  principally  used  for  the  roofs  ot  houses ; and  on  account  of  the 
estaUished  rule  amongst  the  Dutch,  of  placing  the  beams  on  the  roofs  of  their  hodses  at  certain  distances, 
the  bo^s  must  be  of  the  exact  length  to  cover  three  beams,  and  exactly  6 feet  long,  at  which  length  they 
are  worth  a rix  dollar  each,  but  if  one  inch  shorter,  only  half  the  value. 

The  sheathing  boards  must  be  all  whole  and  unbroken  pieces;  the  double  boards  arc  preferable  to  the
