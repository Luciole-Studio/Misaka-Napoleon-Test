The Tea Duties, and the Amount of Bullion consigned to India for the purchase of 
Hemp on account of Government, not requiring observation, the only remaining 
head of the Expenditure on which explanation seems requisite, is the sum paid to 
the Captains of Ships worn out in the Service, amounting to £. 355,190. ‘This 
Payment is connected with the Charge for Freight. It is a temporary expense, as 
it began in 1796-7, and ended in the year 1803-4, and was occasioned by an altera- 
tion adopted by the Company in their Shipping system, about the year 1795. _-Ante- 
cedent to this period, a presumptive right to the commands of the Chartered Ships in 
the service, as to a species of Property, had been created by continued usage, so 
that when a Ship had run the number of voyages stipulated in the Charter-party, and 
became unfit for service, a claim was kept upon the part of the Owners to supply 
another Ship in her stead, and of the Captain to have the command of it when so sup- 
plied. ‘The abolition of this claim was necessary to the substitution of the new system, 
which rested upon the principle of open competition, and it was thought equitable on 
abolishing it, to make a proportioncd compensation to the Captains, which in the 
aggregate amounted to the sum above stated. A considerable saving of expense was 
anticipated from the measure.
