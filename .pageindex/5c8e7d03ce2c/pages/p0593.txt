No. 5. 

A Catcutation, shewing at what time a ship must sail from England, and arrive in and depart 
from India, so as to perform Eight successive Voyages, without intermission, in the shortest 
possible time, 

THE data assumed for the Calculation are these = 

‘That three weeks be allowed for the receipt of the cargo outward. 

That if the ship sails from England in the favourable season of the year, her passage shall be 
considered to consume from four to five months, say, the mediuin of four months and a half; and 
in the unfavourable season, five months. 

‘That six:weeks be allowed for the landing of the Europe, and the receipt of the Indian cargo. 

That if she is dispatched home inthe unfavourable season of the year, her passaze be con- 
sidered the same as in the voyage outward, at five months; or in the favourable season, four 
months and a half, 

That three weeks be allowed for the delivery of the cargo homeward ; and, 

That one week be allowed for surveying, and making sny trifling repairs she may be found to 
stand in need of, previous to her departing upon an ensuing voyage.
