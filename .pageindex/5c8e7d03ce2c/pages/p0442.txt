The Trade from India is from its nature far more liable to be affected by contin- 
gencies than that from China, and especially from the circumstance of a very ma- 
terial part of the goods being purchased at the Sales in England for exporta- 
tion. The operations of the Act of 39th of Geo. III. already adverted to, for 
warehousing the goods, may be supposed to have produced some effect upon the 
Gross Produce of the Sales, which in the Estimate of 1793, and till the year 
1798-9, included the whole of the Customs payable upon the goods. The extent 
of the Sale Amount trom the years 1793-4 to 1796-7 considerably exceeded the 
Estimate; it fell below it in 1797-8, but in the next year it was more in a twofold 
degree, and from 1799-1800 to 1801-2 a considerable excess was maintained ; frem 
that period till the year 1808-9 a diminution is exhibited, but in 1809-10, the last 
ycar of the Account, a revival again appears. 


The extent of the Sale Produce of goods from India on the seventeen years, is 
on the average £. 2,637,746, exceeding the Estimate by £. 322,846. 


The Amount of the Sales of goods from China does not exhibit so much fluc- 
tuation as that from India. In three years, 1793-4, 1796-7, and 1797-8, it was 
below the Estimate ;. but in 1794-5 and 1795-6 it was above, and trom 1798-9 
to 1809-10 the excess was considerable in each year. On the average of the 
whole seventeen years, the Amount was £. 3,405,663, being more than estimated by 
47325263. 

‘The Gross Produce of the Sales was estimated in 1793 at £. 4,988,300, as before 
stated. The average of the seventeen years was £. 6,043,409, which exceeds the 
estimated Amount in the sum of £. 1,055,109. ‘ 


In the Estimate of 1793 the calculation of the Prime Cost of goods was made 
upon a rate of exchange different from that in the Estimate with which it is to be 
compared; and the Charges are proportioned at six instead of five er cent. upon 
the Sale amount, as in the latter Estimate; and considering that the comparison 
proposed could. not be correctly made without some alteration, Your Committee 
have caused an Estimate to be prepared, calculating the Prime Cost at the rates of 
exchange, and the Charges according to the principle required.
