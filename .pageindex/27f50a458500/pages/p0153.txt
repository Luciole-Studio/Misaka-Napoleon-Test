modest, army garrison. Despite these measures, Gómez needed only a
few days’ bombardment carried out by two well-positioned mountain
guns to capture the town on 24 October 1836; the Carlists charac-
teristically employing night attacks to secure parts of the town. The
Military Governor of Almadén, Aranguren, surrendered the town after
its general destruction had served as morbid battle honours. Aranguren,
displaying a non-committal rhetorical ability common to Cristino elites,
made a parting plea to Gómez not to destroy the valuable mercury
mines as these would be vital for national prosperity ‘whoever wins
this war’.98
   The occupation of Almadén was the ﬁnal straw for Rodil’s position.
An image appeared in the press lampooning him: showing him wearing
an eyeglass to survey the campaign horizon, whilst Gómez’s expedition
passed between his extended legs.99 Laughter damaged reputations in
cultures of honour and Rodil was sacked both from the Royal Guards
and the War Ministry. Gómez, for his part, evaded capture yet again, and
on 31 October marched into Cáceres (Extremadura), beginning a three-
day occupation of this provincial capital which had been abandoned to
its fate by its civil authorities and Militia.
