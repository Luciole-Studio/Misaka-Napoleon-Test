   Meanwhile, anti-marotistas may have been cowed but they were not
inactive. Propaganda was spread describing Maroto as a traitor prepared
to sell the King for English gold, and starting rumours that Carlist defec-
tors, contrary to being welcomed into Cristino ranks, were being shot.100
An exchange of letters between the apostólico priest, Juan Echevarría,
and Maroto, revealed a personal antipathy which typiﬁed the mali-
cious gossip that had dominated Carlist court politics over six years.
Maroto, whose impressment into his personal service in 1835 of a cap-
tured adolescent British auxiliary had been the subject of apostólico
gossip and innuendo, now vengefully repaid his tormentors in kind,
waving off Echevarría’s accusations of treason and accusing him of hav-
ing fathered children, and of trying to hide the affair by forcing their
unhappy mother to marry her brother.101 The puritanism of Carlist
political culture was being cast off by its own elites.
   On 9 August, the late Zumalacárregui’s right-hand man, Zaratiegui,
led four battalions in a pro-war pronunciamiento near Pamplona.102 But
this rising was deadlocked: both marotistas and apostólicos competed to
win over the vital legitimacy of Don Carlos, but the King now had grave
doubts about his personal situation in the wake of Maroto’s coup. When
he reviewed some Basque battalions at Elorrio, rather than shouting the
customary ‘Long Live the King’, the men cried ‘Long Live Peace!’103 This
civil war within a civil war was sapping the war effort from within.
General Elío was caught between the Scylla of Cristino invasion and
the Charybdis of the apostólico rising, remarking ‘If I attack the ris-
ing, we are lost, and if I don’t, then I see no hope.’104 This scenario
revived the fortunes of the much-derided Muñagorri who linked up with
Jaúregui on the frontier under the ever-more attractive banner of peace
with fueros without Don Carlos.105 Knowledge was widespread that a
transacción along these lines was the subject of negotiations, especially
at Bayonne.106
