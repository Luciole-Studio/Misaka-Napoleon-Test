Carlos prevaricated and reopened the miguelista-agraviado conspiratorial

escaped to join his King in Portugal after Ferdinand’s death.
   The Pretender’s ﬂight galvanised rather than stymied Carlist conspir-
acies. Arms were forged in convents and monasteries, illicit deliveries
took place between Portuguese miguelistas and Carlists in northern
Spain, whilst even a prospective ‘pan-legitimist’ uprising planned to
link Marseilles and southern France with northern Spain on the occa-
sion of Ferdinand’s death. Whilst Llauder purged Carlists from the army,
Prime Minister Cea Bermúdez, by contrast, pursued a juste milieu policy,
hoping, like any enlightened technocrat, that the ‘nonsense’ of Carlist
versus Liberal violence would cancel itself out.60 Whilst Llauder’s mil-
itary dictatorship was allowed to run its course, Cea hoped that his
crash reforms in the way the state was organised would suffocate the
warring factions ‘from above’. Cea’s civilian absolutism thus rivalled
the regional dictatorships of the Cristino generals. The February and
November 1833 municipal reforms were carried out by force, reforming
town halls to ensure that they would be under the sway neither of tax-
payers as during the Triennium, nor of local Royalist (Carlist) hierarchies
as during the Ominous Decade. Henceforth, town halls would represent
only ‘respectable’ local interests. (The latter were to be elected only on a
