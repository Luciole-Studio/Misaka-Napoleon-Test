59. Another factor that may have contributed to a fall in marine insurance premiums was
    an increase in competition among marine insurers after new marine insurance compa-
    nies entered into the market from 1824. Had this been the case, we would expect to see
    a general fall in marine insurance premiums for British textile exports to all markets.
    Premium data for cargoes from Britain to continental Europe, however, show that
    premium rates remained at a similar level before and after 1824 (between 1 and 1.25
    per cent the invoice value of cargoes). Nevertheless, it is likely that there was not one
    marine insurance market for British textile exports, but many: a market for cargoes to
    nearby locations (e.g. continental Europe) and other markets for distant outlets, such
    as the Southern Cone. Consequently, it could still be the case that competition after
    1824 pressed down on premium rates to distant markets (e.g. Chile), but I can find no
    evidence to confirm this.
