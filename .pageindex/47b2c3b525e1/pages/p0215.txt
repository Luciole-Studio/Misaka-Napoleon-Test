  societies than his papers reveal; and there is no doubt that towards the end of the war
  the societies tended to look to Britain for guidance, whether they got it or not. But
  in the absence of stronger evidence it seems reasonable to surmise that Bentinck never
   had any direct or reliable contact with the Italian patriots until he himself went to
   Italy in 1814; that this situation was practically inevitable given the state of communi-
   cations between Sicily and the Continent; and that his belief in the Italians’ desire and
   capacity to rise against the French was based much less on any knowledge of what
   was actually going on in Italy than on his own hopes and intuitions.
