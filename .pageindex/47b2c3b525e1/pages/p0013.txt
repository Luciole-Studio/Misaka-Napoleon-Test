                            PREAACE

This book—there is no disguising it—started out as a dissertation
for the Ph.D. degree in the University of Cambridge, and later
turned into a Thirlwall Prize essay. But I have revised and in
part rewritten it for publication. As it stands it rests on a good
deal of earlier work by British and Italian historians, notably
Sir Charles Webster’s great work on Castlereagh. But I have
tried to tell from the point of view of both countries the story
of events which have to do at once with diplomatic and with
local political history, as well as—in their abortive way—with
the history of British colonial expansion.
   His Grace the Duke of Portland allowed me to use Lord
William      Bentinck’s   papers, now   on loan to Nottingham
University Library. Professor Walter Maturi, of Turin Universi-
ty, let me read his transcript of King Ferdinand’s Diary, which
together with the other Bourbon papers in the Naples State
Archives was destroyed in 1943; he also gave me much helpful
advice. Miss H. M. Lackland (Mrs F. Best) lent me books and
notes which she had made for her earlier study of the subject.
The late Senator Benedetto Croce also let me see copies of
documents in his possession. Professor Gaetano Salvemini read
an early version of the MS., questioned a number of points, and
so led me to do all I could to strengthen my argument. Mr G.
Ellis Flack, librarian, and Miss J. Renshaw, archivist of Notting-
ham University Library, gave me much practical help; so did
Mr R.H. Ellis and other officials of the Public Record Office,
and Dr E. Librino and Dr L. A. Pagano of the Palermo State
Archives. I have also had advice and encouragement from
Mr C. W. Crawley, Mr D. Mack Smith, and others. I am
thankful to them all. What I owe the Master of Peterhouse the
dedication tries to say.
