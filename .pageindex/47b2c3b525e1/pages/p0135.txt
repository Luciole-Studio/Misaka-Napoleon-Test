                    THE   CONSTITUTION         FAILS                  IIQ
   ‘Sicily’, he wrote to Castlereagh, ‘can only be saved from political
convulsions by our interference. As the Constitution takes effect and as
the liberty of the Press and their new institutions make an impression
upon the opinion and character of the people, it may be hoped that
more correct sentiments of private and public honour will prevail. The
country must rapidly increase in wealth and strength—all the obstacles
opposed by the feudal system and a bad government to the prosperity
of the agriculture and commerce of the country are rapidly removing—
they are clamorous for all the advantages of freedom but nobody will
submit to the sacrifices—nobody will pay or serve. This is shortly the
state of the country. It may become powerful and independent if the
constitution succeeds and the nation is true to itself—but if not, and in
the interval of its infancy and weakness, it must be the province of Arcot
with its Nabob, which if not governed by us, will be by our enemy.
The management required is to make the interposition of this influence
the general object of desire both of the Prince and of the People.’*
