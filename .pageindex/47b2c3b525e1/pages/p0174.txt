                       APPENDIX*A

WILLIAM        DRUMMONDS MISSION                    TOSICI™N
                     1806-1808

While the peace negotiations of 1806 were still going on, Fox
had reminded Holland of ‘the great importance of Sicily, which
you, young one, very much underrate’.* After the failure of
the negotiations, the Grenville Ministry had to decide on a
long-term Sicilian policy. Neither Sicily nor its King could be
abandoned to French aggression; but something had to be done
to counteract the ‘unfortunate’ and ‘disgusting’ behaviour of
the Court, to which the outgoing Minister, Hugh Elliot, attri-
buted the loss of Naples.* Besides, Grenville himself considered
that England must continue to protect Sicily and Sardinia after
a peace, ‘and thus make of all these islands in the Mediterranean
an important chain of stations highly useful to us both in war
and in commerce’.*
   The new Minister, William Drummond, was accordingly
sent out with instructions to conclude a treaty giving Britain,
among other things, the right to garrison Messina in peacetime,
and the commercial status of most-favoured nation. He was to
secure Sicily’s fullest cooperation in the war: therefore, Howick
wrote, ‘whenever you may see the common safety endangered
by the operation of causes likely to defeat this necessary end,
you will not fail to interfere with the whole weight of your
authority and influence. ... Observing upon all occasions the
respect which is due to an ancient and independent government,
you will endeavour... to prevent any waste of the resources
which Sicily can furnish for its own defence; whether it shall
arise from improvident laws and privileges, or from the cor-
ruption of the public authorities.   ... The large sacrifices which
His Majesty has made and continues to make for the support of
the King of the Two Sicilies gives you an undisputed right to
interpose with your advice, even if that right were not to be
found in the consideration which you owe to the interests of
His Sicilian Majesty Himself.’ The resources of ‘so fertile an
