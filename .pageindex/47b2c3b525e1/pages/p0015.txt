When the Bourbons were driven out of Naples in 1860 they left
behind one collection of State and private papers and took another
with them into their Bavarian exile. The first collection, after
having been made public only in 1930, was destroyed in 1943.
The second has now been bought by the Italian State and returned
to the Naples State Archives. Most of it-was there made available
to students in the late summer of last year, when this book was
already in page-proof. Through the courtesy of Count Filangieri,
Superintendent of the Naples Archives, I was able on a recent
visit to read some of the papers and to go through the inventory.
Much of the collection, as it turns out, duplicates that which was
lost in 1943 (and which several historians had previously studied).
The new material concerning the Napoleonic period seems likely
to yield some interesting details rather than to alter anyone’s
interpretation of important matters.
