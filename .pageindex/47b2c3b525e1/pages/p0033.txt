strengthened the patriotic party, especially among the Barons,
disunited since the last Parliament. Belmonte’s house became
the headquarters of a group including not only the reformers
but also a number of conservative Barons: Orleans gave them
Open support. On March Ist a majority of the Peers then in
Palermo addressed a petition to the delegate body which was
supposed to watch over the enforcement of laws between
Parliamentary sessions—the Deputation of the Realm. They
asked the Deputation to remind the Crown of the ancient and
unquestioned privileges of Parliament; protested against the
Decrees; and asserted, nevertheless, their willingness to provide
all necessary supply by constitutional means.* The Deputation
dodged the petition for some weeks by failing to meet. But
delay only increased the political tension. Amherst, about to
leave, feared ‘the most fatal consequences’. The Decrees, he
foresaw, would not solve the Court’s financial problems, while
‘general discontent’ would certainly be produced, and ‘ultimate
resistance’ might perhaps be expected.*
  It was at this point that the Foreign Secretary, Lord Wellesley,
offered the mission to Lord William Bentinck.
