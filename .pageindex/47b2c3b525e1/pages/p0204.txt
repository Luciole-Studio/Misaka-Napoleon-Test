  (Castelcicala reminded the British Government that they did not care much for
  Parliamentary Reform at home). Webster (i, 78) says the King first suggested the
  English Constitution. This is a plausible interpretation of Bentinck’s despatches (see
  p. 49 and note), but it is contradicted by Bentinck’s Journal and by the King’s own
  Giornale d’ Affari, which Webster could not have seen. In fact the King went on
  denying his son’s right to effect any kind of reform through Parliament. Only in
  August 1812 did he start insisting on the pure English Constitution—after Parliament
  had voted the Bases of the new Constitution. The deep-laid schemes which Webster
   attributes to him were not his but the Prince’s (see p. 50). All constitutions were
   anathema to the King; if he later clung to the English it was as a pis-aller.
