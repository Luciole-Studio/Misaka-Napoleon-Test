a ‘posthumous conquest of Napoleon’. L. Blanch, ‘Luigi de’ Medici’, 166. By the decrees of
  December 1816 Ferdinand changed his title to Ferdinand I, King of the Kingdom
  of the Two Sicilies; the whole existing system of local government was abolished,
  and seven new provinces set up each under an Intendente, with new municipal councils
  to be nominated by the King. Conscription was introduced. The annual revenue
  was fixed at the 1813 level (which, however, had included the British subsidy); any
  increases would have to be ‘approved by Parliament’. Previously, the Sicilian flag,
  the separate Sicilian army, and the freedom of the Press had been abolished: Bianco,
  278-94.
