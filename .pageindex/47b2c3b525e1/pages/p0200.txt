agents continued to infest her antechamber. StClair hinted to Orleans and Belmonte that
  the Queen would consent to a Parliament in Sicily and a Constitution for Naples
  if the King was allowed to resume: Journal,     1 Mar. 1812.
appealing to Austria and Russia for help. These rumours were the staple of conversation
  at Palermo—e.g. ‘reports in circulation of Guillotines ordered by the Queen...
  strange reports current in the town of the intention of the Queen to confine me and
  Lady William, Lord and Lady Montgomerie and Gen. McFarlane’: Journal, 27 Jan.
  1812. They appear in a mass of informers’ reports (particularly those of J. Badge
  and the Abate Milano, the Queen’s librarian) among the Bentinck papers. There is
  no telling whether Bentinck paid any attention to these reports, which were, like
  most of their kind, inaccurate and tendentious.
