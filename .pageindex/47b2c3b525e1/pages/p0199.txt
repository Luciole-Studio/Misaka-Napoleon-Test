an ‘ambitious and ungrateful son’.   Journal, 27 Apr., 31 Aug. 1812; Capograssi, 87-9.
  The King filled his Journal, from the very start of the Vicariate, with complaints
  of his son’s disobedience. The Queen cared little who heard her aspersions on the
  Prince’s conduct—‘Bete qu’il est—she cursed him in all languages’: Journal, 18 Apr.
  1812. Bentinck was astonished to learn that the Prince continued to write affectionate
  letters to his mother, often twice a day, when ‘she hates him, he fears her!’: Journal
  23 Apr. 1812. He could not understand this peculiarly Mediterranean kind of family
  feeling, which could combine mutual exasperation with mutual devotion, and made
  the Prince—a    man in his thirties, himself a father—as submissive as a child before
  his parents.
