   after the 1813 Parliament, not before, that the Constitutionalists began to feel the
   need of a stronger executive power. Some of the changes proposed by Balsamo are
   curious: he suggests that taxes should be divided into perpetual (‘independent from
   any parliamentary grant’), ordinary (army estimates), and extraordinary (emergencies)
   —only the latter two kinds to be voted by Parliament; and then, after this return to
   the old Sicilian Constitution, he goes on to say that ‘the Parliament must retain the
   full power of altering or changing any perpetual or ordinary tax’!
