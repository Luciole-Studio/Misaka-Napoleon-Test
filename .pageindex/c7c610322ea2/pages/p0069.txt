   The congregation of the Oratoria de S. Felipe de Nery, to which Verney
belonged, had taken the lead in Portugal, as elsewhere in Catholic Europe,
in the introduction of scientific experimentation and in the conflict with
the Jesuits over pedagogical models. The Oratorians were strong promot-
ers of the natural sciences, and they also stressed the importance of the
Portuguese language, grammar, and orthography, which they believed
should be studied directly and not via Latin. The reason the Jesuits were
the butt of this criticism was obvious: they held a monopoly of higher edu-
cation in Portugal and of secondary education in Brazi1. 5
