   Paradoxically the slave revolt in the West Indies had added acuteness to
the sugar planters' demands for freedom from government interference
and control, just as the events in Saint-Domingue had stimulated appre-
hensions about the racial balance of the population, and produced the so-
cioeconomic situation out of which the Bahian conspiracy emerged.
