   John Quincy Adams and Henry Chamberlain were both right. The
characteristics and options that had marked the long eighteenth century in
the Spanish and Portuguese Atlantic had been permanently transformed.
Yet this had occurred at least in part because the options for two very im-
portant participants in the eighteenth-century South Atlantic world, Spain
and Portugal, had been largely superseded and destroyed. For them, the
long eighteenth century had ended.
