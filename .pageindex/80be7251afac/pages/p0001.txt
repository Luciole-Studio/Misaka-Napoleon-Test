Baring Brothers is the oldest merchant
bank in the City of London, and one of the
most pre-eminent British financial
institutions. Beginning as traders and
manufacturers in Exeter, the Baring
family established a merchant house in
1762, and — over two hundred years later —
the founder’s direct descendants are still
deeply involved in its management. Its
history is also the history of merchant
banking in Britain, for Barings blazed the
trail along which so many have since
followed. But it must also first and
foremost bea history of members of the
family themselves. What enabled them to
survive through so many vicissitudes and
changing circumstances?
In less than a century Barings had soared
through trading in commodities and
accepting bills to financial heights from
which the operations of ordinary
commercial life looked small and distant.
They specialized in the issue of loans for
overseas governments, and the house’s
influence was such that in 1818 Barings
could be described by the Duc de
Richelieu as the sixth power in Europe
after Britain, France, Austria, Prussia and
Russia. They owned country houses and
huge estates, their picture collections
would have merited a sizeable gallery, and
between them they acquired four peerages
before 1900. Beyond and behind all this
lay a position and a reputation such as no
other British merchant bank has ever
achieved.
