  There were other ways in which the Germans showed a
progressiveness foreign to the British manufacturer. In the
matter of candlesticks, for instance, the Germans made a light
weight, showy article which came within the tariff minimum
exemptions, while the British article by its old-fashioned solidity
exceeded the weight limit; consequently German agents sold
candlesticks and the English firms lost the market. Moreover,
the German and American houses were content with smaller
profits if they could secure the market for their goods, while
the British agent demanded larger returns.82 Sheffield knives,
packed without oiling, often arrived too rusty to be salable,
