605 in the second half of 1812.!?7 No ostensible recovery

Sainte-Marie’s consultative chamber sank to new levels of

Elsewhere, it seems, recovery during 1812 gave way to reces-
sion in 1813. The local reports from Belfort, Guebwiller,
Wesserling,     Munster,        Bollwiller, Cernay, Masevaux,              and the
Logelbach all emphasize that industrial employment, produc-
tion, and sales in 1813 were well down on the previous year’s
returns.!°° The metal industries of the department were in a
similar recession. The prefectoral reports for 1812 and 1813
point to a stagnation, or worse, in total production from
the early months of 1811,13! and further upsets were to come
with the Allied invasion. Altogether, then, as far as the offt-
cial record is concerned, the Continental Blockade in Alsace
ended where it had begun: in an economic environment of
prevailing gloom. In spite of happier times in between, it
appears that the wheel had come full circle. A critical re-
appraisal of the same official record, however, leaves us with
a different overview of the whole cycle and its effects.
