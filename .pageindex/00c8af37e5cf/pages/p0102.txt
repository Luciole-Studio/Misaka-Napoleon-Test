82     ALSACE     AND THE CENTRAL           GOVERNMENT           1803-1807

   The chief spur to recovery in the cotton industries of the
Haut-Rhin between 1800 and 1806, in spite of so many
advertised inhibitions, was the growth of the home market.
For the Mulhousiens in particular, this was the master-key to
higher production. For the department in general, a drop in
the number of individual establishments had been out-
weighed by an expansion of the active installations within
each. In other words the cotton industries, particularly the
branch specializing in printed calicoes (totles peintes or
indiennes), had become more concentrated. According to
some sources,!*° output in Mulhouse during the Year XII
(September 1803 to September 1804) had begun to approach
the level of 1786-8 for the first time.!*° The evidence here is
contradictory, however, as Desportes believed that there had
been a decline in the crucial calico-printing sector during the
same year,!?” due to maritime losses and the competition of
British manufactured goods in Continental markets. He
thought that the decree of 22 February 1806 had worsened
matters by reducing supplies of essential primary materials
(toiles blanches) for printing. While his evidence of a fall in
employment between September 1803 and January 1806
is clear, he did not though establish the decline in terms of
output. Indeed, the statistical statement of 29 April 1806 is
at variance with his later reports, notably that of 17 January
1807.28 Altogether, it is far from certain that 1804 and 1805
were depressing years for the leading indienneurs of Mul-
house and of the Haut-Rhin more generally. If there was a
downturn, it came not during the Year XII but after the
decree of 29 February 1806.
