of Bellefontaine in the arrondissement          of Porrentruy

metric quintals, of which 5,000 were wrought iron. Rochet
of Audincourt needed 400 workers to achieve an annual
production of 6,000 metric quintals of cast iron, a figure
Antonin & Vieillard of Belfort could reach apparently with
only 308. The same tables give the department’s total work-
force in these industries as 1,532. Total output was then
29,000 metric quintals of cast and 13,700 of wrought iron.
   Of the other industrial branches, Desportes stressed the
importance of clock- and watch-making, on which the Haut-
Rhin relied for much of its foreign earnings. Some 4,000
craftsmen and others were employed in it during 1806, while
its visible trade was worth at least 200,000 francs annually
and its total value perhaps as much as a million.'*” Japy’s
