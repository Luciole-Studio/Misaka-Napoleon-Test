the immediate needs of their weaving works, just as others
had their own weaving installations to supply their printing
tables. Desportes’s record of 29 April names only two spin-
ning enterprises of any size: Lischy Dollfus of Bollwiller and
Gros, Davillier & Roman. Both also had important weaving
works, and the Wesserling firm was the Haut-Rhin’s foremost
producer of printed calicoes. As an enterprise founded in the
July preceding Desportes’s inquiry, Lischy Dollfus had not
yet had time to establish a pattern of production. In January
1806 it had seven active mule-jennies, together operating
1,404 spindles, and 72 employees. Its anticipated annual out-
put of yarn was given as 13,000 to 14,000 kg, plus anything
up to 10,000 plaincloth articles suitable for printing. The
expansion of the spinning works at Gros, Davillier & Roman
had been stimulated by the firm’s own need for yarn. Com-
