   The industrial record of the Bas-Rhin between 1800 and
1806 also does not square with the Chamber’s more descrip-
tive accounts, while the official statistics are not always
consistent within themselves. The main manufacturing
branches     can   be    identified     as   cottons,     woollens,   linens,
primary and secondary metallurgy, and all undoubtedly
recovered from the later Revolutionary losses during those
years. As the Chamber itself noted on one occasion,!!3 the
iron foundries were ‘flourishing’ and fully active. If labour
and combustibles could be had at a reasonable price, the ex-
pansion of these enterprises was assured. Mechanization had
been extended in the larger textile firms, though on only a
modest scale when compared with other imperial enterprises.
   The industrial headway had been concentrated chiefly in
Strasbourg, and most conspicuously in the cotton-spinning
works of Jean Malapert, who had started this business in
December 1801. Shée’s statistical tables and accompanying
comments      on the cotton        industries     of the Bas-Rhin,     dated
30 March and compiled in response to the ministerial cir-
cular of 30 January 1806, testify to Malapert’s progress.!!4
The first manufacturer to introduce machine spinning into
the department, he had increased his mule-jennies from 6 to
14 and his teasel-heads (cardes) from 4 to 8 between Septem-
ber 1803 and January 1806. During the same period his
work-force    of men, women,          and children had risen from 102
to 160, and in the latter year his expected output was given.
as 8,000 kg of yarn. The prefectoral tables also refer to the
enterprises of Bronner and J.-H. Stupfel as noteworthy in
cotton spinning. Both were based in Strasbourg, and the
latter had set up business undaunted in 1789. They owned
no jennies and from the official description seem to have
operated mainly on the putting-out system. Their fortunes
over the years 1803-6 apparently changed little, and their
combined employment is given invariably as 700 men,
women, and children, who worked with small spinning
wheels and were widely dispersed around Strasbourg. Their
combined output was estimated at about 16,000 kg a year.
