    Desportes’s statistical details show none the less that the
 fortunes of the Haut-Rhin’s hosiers had improved between
 September 1803 and January 1806. Leydecker & Caesar of
 Sainte-Marie, the most prominent firm, had increased the
 number of active installations from 70 to 83 and their work-
 force from 210 to 250 during those years. None of the six
 other producers named employed more workers, though the
 unnamed twenty-two manufacturers of Sainte-Marie who
 worked on their own account and on a modest scale may
 have had a higher combined output. Collectively, they were
 credited with a net annual value of 10,000 francs, as against
 the estimate of 8,900 francs for Leydecker & Caesar. Taken
 as a whole, the hosiers had increased their active installations
 by nearly 72 per cent between September 1803 and January
 1806. In the same period their total employment had risen
 from 457 to 801 persons, or by more than 75 per cent, and
 the net annual value of production at the latter date was put
 at 41,500 francs. Though of course a net figure, this was only
