The industrial reports on the Bas-Rhin during the period
under discussion here have the same pitfalls as the commer-
cial record. Their main drawback is incompleteness, itself
due to the lack of adequate time for collecting all the in-
formation required. Some of the inaccuracies were so glaring
that the ministers more than once rebuked Lezay-Marnésia
for laxity.     Yet one      must,     of course,       make   the best of the
reports,    for otherwise it would be impossible to generalize
for the period at all. The two main documentary collections
which matter here are the prefectoral tables for the Bas-Rhin
dated May 1812 and February 1813°’ and Brackenhoffer’s
mayoral tables for Strasbourg itself, variously dated as 20
March, 16 June, 25 August, and 8 December 1812, and 13
February, 22 April, 6 July, and 7 October 1813.°° These give
half-yearly estimates of the number of active installations and
of output and employment levels in the various industrial
branches between January 1810 and, in most cases, June
1813. Whatever their inadequacies, there is no other com-
parable statistical guide to the Bas-Rhin’s industrial fluctua-
tions in the period.
   Taken as a whole, these documents suggest a slow and
uneven recovery from the crisis, especially in Strasbourg.
