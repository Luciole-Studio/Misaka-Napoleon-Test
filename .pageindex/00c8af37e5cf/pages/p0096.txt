     The   cotton    hosiers of the Bas-Rhin, of whom             sixteen are
named in Shée’s tables and others referred to collectively,
had no outstanding leader. Variously based in Strasbourg,
Molsheim, and Bischwiller, they worked either on their own
account or with at most a handful of apprentices. All, it
appears, were supplied in yarn by Malapert, and none was
credited with a profit exceeding 2,400 francs a year. Three
admitted to net gains of over 1,000 francs, but the rest had
returned figures varying from 780 to as little as 88 francs.
Altogether, this branch of the department’s cotton industry
was    based   on small-scale, domestic workshops, of some                  col-
lective importance but without magnates. Six of the named’
houses     dated     from   before   the    Revolution,   three      from   the
1790s, and the rest had started later. While most had pushed
up production during 1804 and 1805, none had enough
capital to mechanize production more ambitiously. In the
nature of their work they were not geared for rapid growth.
