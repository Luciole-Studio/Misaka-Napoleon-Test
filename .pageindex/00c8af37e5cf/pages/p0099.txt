   Yet perhaps the biggest gap in Shée’s table lies in primary
metallurgy. Presumably the important firm of Dietrich & Cie,
the largest ironworks in the department, is included under
the ‘usines’ of that description, though ‘forges’ and ‘four-
neaux’ might have stated its activity more accurately. It had
lost its titular head through execution during the Terror,
and   though      still nominally      owned      by Dietrich’s        widow,
was in fact managed throughout the Napoleonic period
by Louis Drion. Based principally in Niederbronn, it had
branches in Reichshoffen, Zinswiller, Jaegerthal, and Baeren-
thal. Another of Shée’s prefectoral tables is more helpful
