This book reopens a subject which has been
virtually unapproachable through substantial
works in English for over fifty years. It deals
mainly with the inland aspects of Napoleon’s
Continental Blockade, and draws widely on
French and German sources to offer a more
general reappraisal of the topic. Its regional
focus is on the frontier province of Alsace (the
departments of the Bas-Rhin and Haut-Rhin),
 where the aims and effects of the Blockade
stand   in interesting   contrast   to the   rather
sombre view from the beleaguered Continental
coastline. It looks beyond the Blockade as a
‘coast system’ or a ‘war machine’ to assess its
importance in the heartland of Napoleonic
Europe as well.
   The author examines the achievements and
reverses of Alsatian trade and industry from the
rupture of the Peace of Amiens in 1803 to the
disintegration of the Blockade in 1813. In so
doing he reveals that the Blockade had more
positive and constructive effects than has
generally been supposed. Confined to the Con-
tinent by the naval superiority of Britain, and
deprived of their colonial markets and sources
of supply, French merchants and manufacturers
were forced back on        the resources of the
Continental mainland.      The French military
successes of 1805-7 seemed to open up new
opportunities for them to conquer Continental
markets beyond the imperial frontiers, just as
they already enjoyed full official protection at
home.
   The author brings up to date the long-
neglected history and historiography of the
Continental system; he significantly modifies
accepted views on the effect of Napoleonic
policy on the French economy during the wars
and their aftermath, and provides an important,
detailed bibliography to serve the needs of
‘specialized researchers in this field.

‘Dr. Ellis is Fellow     and   Tutor   in Modern
‘History at Hertford College, Oxford.
