   Another interesting detail of Desportes’s evidence is that
between 1786 and 1788 the value of articles imported into
Mulhouse’ had exceeded that of its exports by nearly 300
per cent. In 1789 the ratio had fallen to 2:1, and in 1790,
for the first time, the value of exports (1,458,000 livres) had
topped that of imports (1,316,888 livres). Altogether, if the
Revolution had had a disruptive effect on the major indus-
tries of Mulhouse, most of the trouble was due to problems
in the French market. Yet the downward trend of sales here,
as well as the steady drop in imported articles, had begun in
1787. Desportes himself admits as much in referring to ‘the
progressive decline’ over the years 1786-90. While he was
right in linking the ruin of several Mulhouse firms with the
Revolution, his own figures point to serious set-backs at least
one year before 1789.
