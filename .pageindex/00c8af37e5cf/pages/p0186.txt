   The fullest statistics for the expansionary period apply to
1808 and 1809. The details of the prefectoral report dated
22 July and of the Chamber’s report to Shée on 5 August
1809 offer us a rough guide to the commercial scale in
question.>! Since these figures were printed without qualifica-
tion in the Annuaire du Bas-Rhin for 1810,** they were pre-
sumably considered officially acceptable. The total volume
of Levantine cotton which passed through Strasbourg in
1808 was given as 9,000 metric quintals, and between 30 and
40 Alsatian carters were engaged in that branch of overland
trade. Total road haulage or roulage, that is the carrying trade
by land in and out of Strasbourg in all commercial branches,
was put at 82,500 metric quintals that year. Nearly three-
quarters of this (60,000 metric quintals) consisted of wines,
and well over a sixth (15,000 metric quintals) of eaux-de-
vie. Some 330 carts drawn by four horses apiece were em-
ployed in this traffic, which came mainly from Burgundy and
the Midi. The two reports are unfortunately not as detailed as
one would like on the trade in colonial goods. All the first
records here is that during 1808 the Strasbourg merchant
houses handled about 4,700 metric quintals of unrefined
sugar and an equivalent amount of coffee and pepper. It is
not even clear whether the latter were intentionally included
under roulage or featured rather as a detached part of river
traffic.
