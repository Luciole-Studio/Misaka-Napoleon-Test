   A very rough idea of scale may be possible from the con-
temporary estimates of population. A prefectoral report for
the last quarter of 1806 gave the total population of Stras-
bourg as 58,000 consumers.®! Some 45,000 were regarded as
permanent residents, another 7,000 as a temporary or
migrant element (précaire), and the local garrison made up
the remaining 6,000. It might be inferred from this simple
basis that the troops represented a little more than a tenth of
the city’s consumer demand. Again, the Bas-Rhin as.a whole
had 14,445 troops in addition to its total civilian population
of over 514,000 during the early years of the Blockade.
This was 2.8 per cent of the total, and the figures for the
Haut-Rhin, where the civilian and military populations were
404,018 and 10,247 respectively at roughly the same time,
show a fractionally smaller percentage.®? Because of their
massive and regular needs, however, it seems likely that the
garrisons consumed a much larger share of local products:
10 or possibly as much as 20 per cent of demand in some
places. Besides all the local garrisons, there was the demand
created by the frequent movement of troops from other im-
perial divisions through Alsace and by the military transports
