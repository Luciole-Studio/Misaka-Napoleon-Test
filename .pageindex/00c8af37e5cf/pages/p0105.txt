Whatever their inadequacies, the available statistics support
the more descriptive evidence of industrial recovery in the
Haut-Rhin during the Consulate and early Empire. Several
quite detailed documents offer an account of the industrial
position of the department shortly before and after the issue
of the Berlin decree. However, they also show that if there
had been recovery and expansion under Napoleonic rule, the
momentum was slowing up in some branches during the last
quarter of 1806 and the first two quarters of 1807. News of
the Berlin decree broke in a context of something like an
industrial slack. As in other imperial departments, notably in
Belgium, the Nord, and Normandy, the trend was most
sharply defined in the cotton industries. It appears that this
wider industrial lull during the winter of 1806-7 had a
slightly earlier effect in the Haut-Rhin. In some cases 1806
was a less satisfactory year than 1805, and in nearly all it
was anticipated that 1807 would be less satisfactory still.
