represented a similar drop. Haussmann fréres had experienced
a fall in output and employment of nearer a third. All their
foreign markets had been lost, and they identified their
difficulty as above all ‘un manque de vente presque absolu’.
Blech-Fries took up the same refrain, alluding in their report
to a ‘prodigious decline’ in sales. Their figures bear this out:
of their regular work-force of 600 in 1805, only 250 had
been active during 1806, and at the time of writing the
number was down to 140. Jérémie Risler of Mulhouse, who
had recently acquired the enterprise of Kielmann & Cie, had
been unable to maintain its former output and employment
levels. Here was one case where current set-backs could be
linked with the loss of transmarine markets; Francois Verdan
of Biel was another.
