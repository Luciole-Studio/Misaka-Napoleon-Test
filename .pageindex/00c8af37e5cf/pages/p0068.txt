   Yet the monopoly was merely a worrying possibility at
the time of the Blockade decrees. The Chamber’s more im-
mediate concern was with the working of existing tobacco
laws. Besides warning the government of the mischief that
further restrictions on imports and exports would bring, it
also protested at the way in which customs officials were
applying existing regulations.°? The ban on the import of
tobacco from Holland continued to give offence to Alsatian
processers and merchants, but repeated requests for permis-
sion to import Dutch leaves and twists were refused.
Another series of petitions emanated from the Chamber
when the issue of an export duty on processed tobacco came
to a head in 1806-7. The decision to impose such a duty of
20 francs the metric quintal was taken in September 1806.
The Chamber bitterly predicted that it would kill the depart-
ment’s tobacco trade completely, since local prices were
