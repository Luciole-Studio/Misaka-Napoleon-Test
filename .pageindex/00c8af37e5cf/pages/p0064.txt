   Thus heartened by earlier prefectoral backing, the Chamber
lost no time in pressing their case to his successor. Writing to
Shée on 7 Floréal XI (27 April 1803),** it claimed that the
annual tobacco yield in lower Alsace before the Revolution
had been between 90,000 and 100,000 metric quintals. From
a fifth to a quarter of the crop had been exported to Swiss
and German clients. Fifteen large and as many lesser enter-
prises had built up their interests in the processing industry,
and an associated trade in imported leaves had also been a
lucrative source of income to local merchants. Then followed
the familiar story of disruption during the Revolution. If
there had been no appreciable drop in the area under cultiva-
tion, indeed rather the opposite, the processers and merchant
expeditioners had certainly been hit. Laumond had estimated
that of the 80,000 metric quintals of processed tobacco pre-
viously exported to Germany, notably to Frankfurt, between
18,000      and 20,000       at most     had been sent there during the
Year IX.*° The greater part of the export earnings formerly
worth around 3 million francs to lower Alsace each year had
been lost. Laumond’s account was reproduced in a long
report written on behalf of the Conseil général d’agriculture,
arts et commerce du Bas-Rhin by Louis Wangen, soon to be-
come the Chamber’s first secretary, and dated 11 Ventdse
XI (2 March 1803).** He added that whereas 36 processing
enterprises in Strasbourg had normally been in service before
the Revolutionary upsets, at the time of writing only 18 were
still active. Their combined employment had dropped from‘
between 8,000 and 10,000 to 3,000 workers.*”
