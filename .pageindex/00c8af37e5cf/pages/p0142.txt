Having thus exhorted the emperor’s Belgian subjects to do
their national duty, de Neufchateau warned of the mis-
adventures of any further infatuation with the industrial
charms       of the    enemy.     Such     flirtation,   he insisted,    would
worsen the baneful effect of the ‘dumping’ of tozles de
coton and mousselines des Indes on the Continent by British
merchants. Already prejudiced by that practice, the rival in-
dustries of the Empire would be turned away completely
from a manufacture based on home-grown primary materials
(flax and hemp) unless the trend to cotton production was
checked. They otherwise faced permanent enslavement to
British competitors in the frivolous cult of cottons.
