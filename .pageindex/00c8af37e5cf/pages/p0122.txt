   Now, in summing up the earlier effects of the Revolution-
ary wars on France, Frangois Crouzet writes that ‘ce qui
manquait & la France au début du Consulat, c’était les dé-
bouchés          extérieurs,    et non    la capacité* productrice,           dont,
malgré les pertes subies pendant la Révolution, une bonne
partie était inemployée.’!®               The point, I think, applies as
well to the Haut-Rhin’s manufacturers at the time of the
Blockade decrees. While they had certainly recovered well in
the years 1800-6 from the turmoils of the 1790s, their
reports of 1806-7 nearly all testify to new anxieties. Default
of markets was plainly foremost among them.
   One notable exception among the twenty-two company
reports of April-May 1807 was the account sent in by the
largest entrepreneur of Mulhouse, Dollfus-Mieg, according
to which the firm had actually progressed during the same
difficult period. In 1806 it had employed 715 persons and
produced 22,000 articles; in April 1807 employment was up
to 1,020 and the production target for that year had been
set    at between          28,000   and 30,000      articles. Expansion        had
been achieved in spite of a total cut-back on exports, for as
the report bluntly put it, ‘notre débouché est en France
seulement’. Clearly the exceptionally good showing of
Dollfus-Mieg had been dependent on widening sales on the
home market.
      On   the       whole,    however,   the manufacturers’         mood      was
none too optimistic in the spring of 1807. Desportes duly
passed on their protests and worries to Champagny, who in
turn informed Napoleon. In his report to the emperor of 18
June 1807 the Minister emphasized the two central problems
of market default and actual surpluses in the Haut-Rhin.!®
Taking Desportes’s lead, he blamed the disruption of foreign
trade, especially of exports to Germany, for those difficulties.
