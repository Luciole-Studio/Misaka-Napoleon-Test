   If L’Huillier is right in thinking that the downturn in the
export of printed calicoes came chiefly after the spring of
 1808, and that their major expansion had come during the
two preceding years, the manufacturers’ own reports during
the important survey of April-May 18079! would be in
doubt. These, more often than not, had testified to decline
and in some cases total suspension of exports to Italy. I am
reluctant to dismiss such detailed and repeated evidence
lightly, for it seems much more likely that the main push into
Italian markets during the expansionary period came between
June 1807 and June 1808. But L’Huillier is sounder on
the point that the Haut-Rhin’s exports to the kingdom of
Italy slackened as from July and especially from October
1808.°? If the prefectoral records show that Dollfus-Mieg
continued to trade there during this tapering-off period,
most other interested ndienneurs found the kingdom a less
easy market during the next two and a half years. They had to
wait until the imperial decree of 10 October 1810 gave it a
new lease of life by creating a still more favourable ‘marché
réservé’ there. It is certain that the expansion of the Haut-
Rhin’s calico-printing industry in the years 1807-10 rested
mainly on the back of the large imperial home market.
