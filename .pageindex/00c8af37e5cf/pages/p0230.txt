One      sudden      impetus,     in a sense       artificially induced,          came
within months of the Berlin decree. As the president of the
trade tribunal in Colmar reported to Desportes on 25 January
1807,*? the wines of the Haut-Rhin then had a higher value
than for many years, and this because of the exceptional
circumstances. The king of Wiirttemberg had given notice of
a stiffer import tariff for foreign wines, and in order to beat
the deadline set, dealers throughout the Swabian lands had
rushed to buy up Alsatian wines at the old duties. Clearly,
such a highly favourable market could not last, but while
sales indeed dropped during the next six months, they re-
mained good on the whole until the economic crisis of 1810-
11. Strasbourg alone had between sixteen and twenty large
mechant houses which normally specialized in the export of
Alsatian and other French wines.*? They were a powerful
local group, collectively as important as the tobacco pro-
cessers,     and like them        sometimes       members           of the Chamber
itself. The problems which chiefly aroused them concerned
customs procedures in Strasbourg and import or transit
duties on French wines across the Rhine.
