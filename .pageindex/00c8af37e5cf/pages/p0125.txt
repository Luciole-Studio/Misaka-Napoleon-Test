   That agriculture was the basis of the Alsatian economy
under Napoleon, measured in terms of gross output values
and numbers employed, would not be seriously disputed.?
Of the Bas-Rhin’s forty-one cantons in 1800, for instance,
thirty-two were almost wholly agricultural. Only the other
nine (Barr, Benfeld, Bischwiller, Geispolsheim, Haguenau,
Niederbronn,  Obernai,  Strasbourg, and Wasselonne) had
manufactories of any note. When the consular government
decided on 3 Nivose XI (24 December 1802) to set up the
new    chambers        of commerce,           and       on   22 Germinal           and   10
Thermidor XI (12 April and 29 July 1803) to create similar
consultative        chambers       for     manufacture,            arts,     and    crafts,
Alsace had few contenders for such recognition. Strasbourg
of course was large and important enough to qualify for a
chamber of commerce,* but it was the only Alsatian town
