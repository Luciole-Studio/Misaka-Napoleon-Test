      ALSACE AND THE CENTRAL GOVERNMENT
   1803-1807: A PORTRAIT OF ECONOMIC DISTRESS?


The Bas-Rhin and Haut-Riin reflect many of the economic
troubles widely current in the French Empire shortly before
and at the time of the Blockade decrees. They also mirror
problems more closely associated with its inland and frontier
regions. This chapter examines the various reactions of
Alsatian merchants and manufacturers to those problems,
analyses the nature of and assesses the validity of their griev-
ances. The primary sources are ample enough but present the
elementary difficulty of trustworthiness. It was common for
local   notables,   economic   commentators,   interested   parties,
and not least the departmental officials to exaggerate the
hardships caused in their regions by the policies of the central
government. One must make due allowance for such over-
statement of local difficulties, such exaggeration for effect.
On the other hand, it was often true that the central govern-
ment    underestimated     and seemed     insensitive to local
problems. Part of the explanation here is that the ministries
in Paris were constantly preoccupied with the sheer weight of
other business, and no department any distance from the
capital could expect special attention all the time. But,
equally, they were genuinely concerned to formulate a
national policy, one that could be applied uniformly in the
departments. Allowances should then be made for such
assimilation of pressing local problems into the routine opera-
tions of the central government.
