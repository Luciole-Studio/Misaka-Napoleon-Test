   And yet it is fair to conclude that if calico-printing was the
Haut-Rhin’s most dynamic economic branch, it was also the
main ‘leashed industry’ at the time of the first Blockade
decrees. As so much of the department’s expansion in the
years 1807-10 was to be achieved in this branch, a closer
inspection of Desportes’s tables of 29 April seems in order.
His identification of and information on the twenty-two
magnates are of crucial importance here.!** The first obvious
point is that these were neither fly-by-night concerns nor the
products of a mushroom growth. It is true that six of the
twenty-two (Dollfus-Mieg, Nicolas Koechlin, Schoening &
Cie, Paul Blech, Petitpierre-Robert,              and Baumgartner & Cie)
were of Napoleonic foundation. All of them, however, had
emanated from established family businesses, and Petitpierre-
Robert had been active in Neuchatel before transferring to
Thann. The remaining sixteen firms all dated from before the
Revolution. If some had been badly stretched by its disrup-
tions, for instance through the liquidation of debts owed
them in depreciating assignats, they had adequate capital to
ride out those troubles. In this respect the industrial
magnates of the Haut-Rhin had an economic security lacking
to those of their merchant brethren in Strasbourg who at the
time had nominally bigger fortunes committed to more
speculative dealings in colonial goods, provisioning, and
smuggling.
