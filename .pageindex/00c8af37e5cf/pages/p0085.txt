   The historical record is also at variance with bland generali-
ties about the Revolution’s disastrous effects on the Alsatian
economy. If Mulhouse suffered without doubt from the
customs disputes of the 1790s, until 1798 it had chosen after
all to stay outside the French Republic. Its plight had been
worsened less by the Revolution as such than by the self-
imposed if understandable struggle for political indepen-
dence. The French commercial blockade of the town in 1796
had brought its crisis to a head and finally forced it to accept
incorporation in the Republic. Yet even Lévy’s generally
favourable account concedes that the cotton industries of
Mulhouse had passed through an unsettled cycle since 1785,
following earlier customs quarrels with the cinq grosses
fermes.®? Indeed, the Alsatian cotton industry as a whole had
been hit by the slump in French textiles in 1787-9, which is
often but not always fairly attributed to the Eden-Vergennes
 trade treaty of 1786.7°
