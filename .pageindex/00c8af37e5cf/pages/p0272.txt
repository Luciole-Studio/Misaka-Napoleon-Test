   The year 1813 was full of troubles for Strasbourg. Even
the retrospective tables of December 1813. which show 1812
as the city’s commercial peak under Napoleonic rule, have
comparatively low figures for it.?? The winter of 1812-13
was by all accounts unusually severe and prolonged. Rhenish
traffic was frequently held up by ice and poor visibility.
As the whole structure of the imperial customs surveillance
deteriorated with the troops away on campaign, smuggling
gained a new lease of life. It may be that the lower trade
figures for 1813 merely disguise a more active illicit traffic,
but this cannot be proved. It seems more likely that the
campaigns of 1813-14 made merchants nervous about dis-
tant transactions and had a depressing effect on foreign
dealings. Like other departments of the Rhenish left bank,
the Bas-Rhin and Haut-Rhin felt the draught of the Allied
military advance some weeks before the imperial army re-
treated across the Rhine. It was then that the negative aspects
of military demands became more prominent, as the monthly
and quarterly reports for 1813 often remark.™
