  We    need, then, to be wary        when    assessing trends in the
widely dispersed context of rural outwork and when linking
those popular supply-lines with their urban markets. For
what    the official estimates are worth, the total of 16,159 is
a considerable advance on the 10,414 employees recorded
for September 1803. The over-all increase here is some 55
per cent, a trend confirmed by the nearly equivalent rise
in the number of active weaving installations from 1,244 to
1,903 during the period. Unfortunately, one cannot plot the
increase in output exactly, since production figures are not
given for the earlier date, but it seems reasonable to infer that
the trend in 1804 and 1805 had also been expansionary. At
any rate, the estimated annual production levels recorded for
January 1806 are 21,760 articles in plaincloth (each of from
18 to 19 metres in length) and a further 28,080 articles classi-
fied as dimities, quilts, and muslins.
