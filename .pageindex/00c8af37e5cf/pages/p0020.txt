                QUOTATION AND TRANSLATION
Apart    from     extracts,   phrases,     and    terms   which   could be
Englished without loss of sense or idiom, quoted French
passages are not translated. The original spelling and punc-
tuation are kept wherever the meaning is clear. Quotations
from German sources are translated, except where the ori-
ginal form (for instance           of technical terms         or idiomatic
phrases) seems preferable.

                   SPELLING OF PLACE-NAMES
Wherever an accepted English spelling of a foreign place-name
is available, this is the usage adopted. Otherwise, the conven-
tion used is official spelling according to the political divisions
of today. Thus towns and other place names within the
present-day frontiers of France keep their French form; while
those which now lie within West Germany, Italy, Belgium,
Holland, etc. appear in the official spelling of these countries.

                                  DATES
Until 1 January 1806 France was administered according to
the Revolutionary calendar. Since many decrees, reports, and
events   are more      familiar in this form, it seems desirable to
keep it. To avoid the inconvenience of frequent consulta-
tion of the Concordance, the corresponding dates of the
Gregorian calendar are given in brackets.
