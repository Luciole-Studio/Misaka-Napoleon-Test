the second half of that year, and the normally active months
from July to October were particularly disappointing.
Customs rigours were partly to blame, as were the higher
imperial droits réunis, which drew reprisals against French
goods entering the Rhenish Confederation. Another con-
tributory factor was a stretch of unusually bad weather
during June, July, and August. Heavy rains made the passage
of goods from the French Midi more difficult and held up
the Rhine dispatches. On 7 July, for instance, nineteen of the
lesser merchants of Strasbourg complained in a petition to
the mayor that ‘perpetual rains’ had spoilt the fair of St.
John and asked, unsuccessfully, for a week’s extension of its
