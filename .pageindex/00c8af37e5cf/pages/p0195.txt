that all these estimates are for legitimate trade. If one tried
to add in speculations for the smuggling trade, in spite of the
hopeless task of quantifying it even approximately, the prima
facie evidence would support 1809 rather than 1812 as the
peak year under Napoleon. The former year was notorious
for contraband dealings everywhere along the inland frontiers
and the coastline, helped by the absence of the troops on the
Wagram campaign. The latter was a year of comparatively
tight customs surveillance, following the Fontainebleau
decree of October 1810, and the clamp-down did not relax
until the French military disaster in Russia.
