   The prefectoral reports for 1809 also make little fuss
about any industrial set-backs during the preceding year.
Indeed in the spring Desportes could claim that both the
spinning and weaving industries had multiplied ‘with striking
rapidity during the last few years’.°4 The government, in issu-
ing the decree of 16 May, had justified the prohibition of
yarn exports partly on the grounds that the spinners had
done well in preceding years and that it was necessary to help
the weavers at a time when supplies of yarn were short. On
the other hand, the ban was lifted by the decree of 30
January 1809,®° though export duties were to be levied, and
the spinners had resumed their sales to foreign clients by the
time Desportes made his report to Paris. His accompanying
tables moreover show a quite sizeable drop in weaving in the
department during the second half of 1808, which was in-
consistent with the aim of the May decrees, and ironically the
statistics for spinning show a slight rise over those months.*
   Again, however, the key indicator of industrial momentum
