Altogether, if one looks back on the Haut-Rhin’s industrial
performance from a point on the eve of the economic crisis
of 1810-11, the preceding three and a half years had seen
unusually fast growth, and of course they followed a period
of recovery and improvement under the Consulate and early
Empire, after the Revolutionary disruptions. Taking 1812
as his base, Chaptal in 1819 placed the Haut-Rhin as the
Empire’s seventh most productive department for cotton
spinning, weaving,!!! and hosiery, while for calico-printing its
only serious rival was Paris.!!2 A contemporary estimate put
the value of total output by the department’s twenty-two
leading indienneurs in 1810 at between 27 and 28 million
francs./!% If reliable, this largely home trade would have
been equivalent to nearly 4 per cent of the Empire’s total
foreign commerce (imports plus exports) that year. On the
assumption that the total number of calicoes printed in the
Haut-Rhin in 1810 was nearer 275,000 than 250,000, the
average value per article would have been around 100 francs.
