   Illicit trade along the Rhenish frontier, particularly in the
upper reaches of Alsace, had more to do with the artificial
barriers imposed on traditional commercial relations there
than with the maritime embargoes.!? While colonial goods
always had an important place in such traffic, other com-
modities like grain, meat, tobacco, and hemp also featured
prominently among illicit shipments. Frangois Crouzet has
commented that smuggling was more active along the inland
than the maritime frontiers of the Empire.!* One reason for
this was the nature of the terrain, with its web of secondary
and tertiary roads through remote areas, its complex of major
and minor rivers, its Rhine crossings and islands, and its wood-
lands and mountains along particularly the Swiss frontier.
Another was the proximity of foreign entrepots like Frank-
furt, Darmstadt, Mannheim, Heidelberg, Rastatt, Kehl,!*
Offenburg, Lahr, and above all Basel. Within the Empire
itself there were many smuggling bases up along the Swiss
frontier and down the left bank of the Rhine.'® Some of the
