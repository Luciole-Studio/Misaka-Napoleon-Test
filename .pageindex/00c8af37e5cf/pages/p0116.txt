   As the prefectoral tables of 17 January are the most con-
temporaneous with the early Blockade decrees, it is useful
to see how they break down by industrial branch. My Table 3
reproduces the essential details. What stands out most plainly
is the vital importance of the cotton industries and siamoises
to industrial employment in the Haut-Rhin. Of the 51,898
industrial employees active in 1806, the five entries for the
cotton branches make up 25,026, nearly half. The real pro-
portion was much higher, since many of the workers included
separately under stamoises were partly or even principally
engaged in cotton weaving. If the applicable figure of 14,981
is added in, the familiar level of just over 40,000 employees
is reached, and it represents over 75 per cent of total indus-
trial employment. Together with the figures for woollens and
the various items under rubanerie, the total of 44,083 textile
workers amounted to about 85 per cent of the department’s
industrial work-force in 1806.
    It would be interesting to know how this compared with
agricultural employment, but alas no serviceable statistics
for the latter are available. We know however from the indus-
trial records that most of the domestic hand-spinners and
weavers were also part-time agricultural labourers, at least
during the spring and summer. Desportes’s figure for the
total population of the Haut-Rhin at the beginning of 1806 is
