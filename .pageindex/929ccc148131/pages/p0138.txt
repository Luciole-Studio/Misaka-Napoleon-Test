Conclusion

       Despite all the efforts of the Horse Guards army recruitment was still

insufficient. But great progress was made in improving recruiting during the period. The

Horse Guards' reforms essentially 'nationalised' the recruiting service, transferring

control of it from the regiment to the state, and was 'well calculated,by the effectual

and immediatechecksit affords to the officer's charges,to guard the public in a great

degree'." All the usual measuresthe army adoptedin wartime servedonly to increase

recruiting fractionally, and the extraordinarysourcesof manpower,although generally

more productive,could not provide the answerto Britain's manpowerproblems.All the

army's remedieshad restrictionsand limits, and all had beenreached.
