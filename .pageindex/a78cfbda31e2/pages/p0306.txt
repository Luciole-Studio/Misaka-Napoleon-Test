   Nor was the rear-admiral the only British officer who had
taken such an idea into his head, grounded upon the same
partial view of the previous surrender. When, in the middle of
the year 1803, intelligence of the declaration of war against
Holland reached Port Boyal, Jamaica, the 10-gun schooner
Gipsy, Acting-lieutenant Michael Fitton, was despatched to
Curacoa, to warn any British cruisers that might be lying there
of what had taken place, in order that they might provide for
their safety. Arriving in the harbour of St. Ann, the Gipsy
found at anchor there the 18-gun ship-sloop Surinam, Captain
Kobert Tucker. To this officer, in as secret a manner as he
could, Lieutenant Fitton communicated the intelligence, and
advised him immediately to get under way. " No," says Cap-
tain Tucker, "I'll summon the fiscal to surrender the island to
me." In vain did the lieutenant represent the folly of such a
proceeding   in vain did he point to the numerous batteries
