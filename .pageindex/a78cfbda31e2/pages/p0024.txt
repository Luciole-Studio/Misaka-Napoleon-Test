  On the 1st of March, in the middle of the night, the British
12-pounder 36-gun frigate Nereide, Captain Frederick Watkins,
cruising off the Penmarcks, discovered to windward five ships
and a schooner. As soon as she had made the necessary pre-
paration for battle, the Nereide hauled up for the strangers;
which, at daylight on the 2nd, were seen to be all armed vessels,
and were then lying to, as if determined to have a contest with
the British frigate. Nor will it be considered that the French
commodore had formed a very rash resolve, when the force of
his squadron is stated.
        The largest ship was the Bellone, of Bordeaux, measuring

and six brass 36-pounder carronades on the quarter-deck and
forecastle  total 30 guns, with a complement of at least 220
