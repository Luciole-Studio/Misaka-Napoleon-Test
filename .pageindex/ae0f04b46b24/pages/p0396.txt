Stratfor.i  on  Avon  : — This  Town  is  par- 
ticularly celebrated  as  the  Birthplace 
of  Shakspeare,  who  died  in  1616 
and  has  a  neat  Monument  in  the 
Church.  The  noted  Mulberry-tree 
which  grew  in  the  Gardens  of  the 
Author  was  destroyed  by  a  Successor, 
and  sold  to  a  Carpenter,  who  inge 
niously  worked  it  into  various  Orna- 
ments and  Trinkets.  Here  in  Sep 
tember  1769  was  the  first  Comme 
moration  of  Shakspeare's  Jubilee, 
when  an  Amphitheatre  was  erected 
on  the  Plan  of  Ranelagh,  for  the 
Reception  of  its  Visitors.  It  lasted 
three  Days,  during  which  Time  every 
Species  of  Entertainment  was  exhi- 
bited ;  such  as  Concerts,  Oratorios, 
Pageants,  Fireworks,  Illuminations, 
&c.  In  the  Guildhall  is  a  large  Room, 
called  Shakspeare's  Hall,  in  which 
are  two  excellent  Paintings  of  Shak- 
speare and  Garrick.  The  Church  is 
a  noble  Building,  with  a  lofty  Spire.
