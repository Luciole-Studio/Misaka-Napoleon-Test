Neath,  the  Neath  Canal :  it  is  united 
with  the  Neath  R.  (S.  of  the  Town), 
which  connects  it  with  Swansea  Bay; 
its  Course  North  is  close  by  the  Neath 
R.  and  extends  to  Abemant.  There 
is  a  Branch  of  some  Length,  West  of 
the  Taff.  Neath  has  two  extensive 
Copper  Works,  and  some  Iron  Forges ; 
and  the  Coal  Pits  in  the  Neighbour- 
hood are  the  Means  of  a  consider- 
able Trade  to  Bristol  and  London, 
the  River  Neath  being  navigable  for 
coasting  Vessels.  About  \  M.  from 
the  Town,  on  the  western  Side  of  the 
River,  are  the  picturesque  Remains 
of  the  Abbey  founded  by  Richard  de 
Granville  and  his  Wife,  for  Cistertian 
Monks  :  in  this  Abbey  Edward  II 
took  Refuge  till  he  was  taken  by  the 
Earl  of  Leicester  in  1326.  It  is  a 
corporate  Town,  and  is  governed  by 
a  Portreeve,  12  Aldermen,  a  Re- 
corder, and  capital  Burgesses.

Swansea  :  the  Town,  built  on  a  semi- 
circular rising  Bank,  is  near  a  Mile 
and  a  Half  in  Length  ;  the  Houses 
are  modern  and  commodious,  and 
the  Streets  are  well  paved.  It  has  a 
Custom  House.  The  Remains  of  its 
ancient  Castle  are  converted  into  a 
Gaol  and  a  Workhouse.  Half  a 
Mile  from  the  Town,  on  the  Beach, 
stands  the  Bathing  House ;  there  are 
also  cold  and  hot  Sea-water  Baths 


near  the  Pottery  on  the  Strand.  The 
Trade  of  this  Town  is  considerably 
improved  by  means  of  the  Swansea 
Canal,  which,  uniting  with  the  R. 
Taff.  close  to  the  Town,  is  con- 
nected with  Swansea  Harbour,  and 
goes  from  thence  by  the  Side  of  the 
Taff  to  Hen  noyadd.  There  is  a 
Pottery  here  upon  Mr.  Wedgewood's 
Plan ;  an  Iron  Foundery ;  and  a 
good  dry  Dock.  It  has  a  consider- 
able Trade  in  the  Export  of  Stone, 
Coal,  Iron  Ore,  and  Limestone,  all 
found  on  the  Banks  of  the  Taff:  there 
are  also  seven  large  Copper  Houses 
some  Distance  from  the  Town,  smelt- 
ing annually  45,00©  Tons  of  Metal, 
and  consuming  70,000  Chaldrons  of 
Coal.  Swansea  is  governed  by  a  Port- 
reeve, 1 2  Aldermen,  2  Chamberlains, 
and  a  limited  Number  of  Burgesses.

Carmarthen  is  a  Place  of  great  Antiqui- 
ty, being  the  Maridunum  of  Ptolemy: 
it  was  once  fortified  with  Walls  and 
a  strong'  Castle,  Part  of  the  Gate 
now  only  remaining  $  and  the  County 
Gaol  occupies  its  Site,  handsomely 
built  of  hewn  Stone.  The  Church 
is  a  very  large  Building,  which,  with 
the  Churchyard,  is  surrounded  by  a 
Wall.  On  the  east  End  of  Priory 
Street  are  the  Ruins  of  a  Priory, 
founded  in  1148.  It  was  first  erect- 
ed into  a  Borough  by  Henry  VIII. 
and  sends  one  Member  to  Parlia- 
ment ;  and  James  I.  granted  it  a 
Charter  of.  Incorporation  as  a  Town 
and  County  of  itself,  with  Power  to 
make  Bye-laws :  it  is  governed  by  a 
Mayor,  6  Justices,  &e.  The  Prince 
of  Wales  has  here  his  Exchequer 
and  Chancery  for  South  Wales. 
There  are  several  Iron  and  Tin 
Mines  in  the  Neighbourhood,  and 
at  the  Entrance  of  Caermarthen  are 
Iron  and  Tin  Mills  belonging  to  John 
Morgan,  Esq.  ;  and  the  Smelting 
House  belonging  to  Lord  Cawdor.
