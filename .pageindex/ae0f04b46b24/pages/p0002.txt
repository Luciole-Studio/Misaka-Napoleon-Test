Anxious  to  preserve  the  good  opinion  of  the  Public,  the  Pro- 
prietor has  used  his  utmost  endeavours  to  correct  those  errors  to 
which  a  publication  of  this  description  must  always  be  liable  j  and 
he  has  so  arranged  the  Remarks  which  accompany  the  Roads,  as  that 
they  and  the  Route  to  which  they  refer,  present  themselves  to  the 
eye  of  the  reader  at  the  same  time. 

To  the  Population,  and  Number  of  Houses  in  each  Town, 
given  from  the  returns  made  to  Parliament — the  Time  of  the  Mail's 
Arrival  at  and  Departure  from  each  Town — the  specification  of 
the  corporate  Towns,  and  of  those  at  which  the  Assizes  are  held 
— the  Course  of  the  Rivers  crossed  on  the  Routes,  and  the  Line
