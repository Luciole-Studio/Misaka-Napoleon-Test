

There was one last irony to this story. When the French occupied Andalucía at the beginning of 1810, Meinadier returned from nearby Puerto Santa María, where he had taken refuge, to Puerto Real. The port and the surrounding countryside had been sacked by the French military of all its remaining grain, straw, charcoal, firewood, olive oil, household valuables and farm equipment. On his arrival, he was acclaimed by the clergy and inhabitants who chose him as local magistrate, a post in which he was confirmed by King Josef. His French heritage now stood him and the inhabitants in good stead; and in his new post he did all in his power to alleviate the population’s distress.27
