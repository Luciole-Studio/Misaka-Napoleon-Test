26‘Recent Writing on Napoleon and his Wars’, Journal of Military History, January 2009, pp. 217–219, which describes Napoleon’s Cursed War, notwithstanding such reservations, as ‘a major contribution to the literature’, which ‘contains a great deal of fresh information on the response of the Spanish people both to the coming of the French and to the total war that they were called upon to fight, whilst at the same time offering much that is new on the social and economic impact of the struggle (in this respect, Fraser’s analysis of the population losses Spain suffered during the war is particularly important, and so far as the current reviewer is aware, unequalled even in Spanish)’. Elsewhere, Esdaile is less generous, proposing two cheers for the book, and contesting in particular its depiction of the rising of 2 May 1808 in Madrid: History, vol. 94, no. 314, April 2009, pp. 247–249. His own account of the same events, however, is too perfunctory to offer any alternative – little more than a page, against Fraser’s vivid and detailed sixteen: compare The Peninsular War, pp. 38–39 and Napoleon’s Cursed War, pp. 56–71.
