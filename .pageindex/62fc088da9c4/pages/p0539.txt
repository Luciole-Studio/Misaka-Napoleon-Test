### 14. ORIGINS OF THE GUERRILLA

*At the turn of the century, of the 75% of the active population engaged in land work, nearly half were renters and one-fifth smallholders and ‘use-owners’, although many of them were little more than subsistence farmers. In Galicia, Vizcaya, Navarra, Aragón and Cataluña about half of the land-working population owned or held the ‘use-ownership’ of their plots. Outside these regions the proportion dropped to between 10 and 25%. (Census of 1797.)

*The Andalucian guerrilla, after the region’s occupation in 1810, began in the same sort of geo-social conditions as in the north: mountainous border terrains in Ronda and Málaga, known for their smuggling activities, and the Alpujarras in Granada, though later it extended widely through the region. La Mancha produced many groups, but they never matched the eventual size of some of those in the north.
