*During the first siege, Palafox’s older brother, marqués de Lazán, and his military advisers had sent an officer with the express mission of forming villagers into corps of guerrillas to prevent supplies reaching the besiegers; experts believed that only five hundred armed men who knew the countryside intimately and whose officers were prepared to take all the necessary measures would have sufficed for the task. But in this first attempt at creating an irregular rural fighting force, nothing was achieved. ‘Disagreements and the incompetence of some officers in the field was, in general, the cause of failure.’ (Lafoz, 1996, p. 152.) *In fact, Moore was disobeying his government’s orders to establish a ‘bridgehead’ in Asturias, a strategically important province, as Napoleon also insisted, since it provided ready access to Galicia to the west, Castilla la Vieja to the south, and the Cantabrian coast centred on Santander to the east; and could easily be supplied by sea from England. If things went desperately wrong, the British navy could evacuate England’s only army in the field without great difficulty. Moore made a serious mistake in not following this plan; but he set little store by the War Office’s strategic thinking, and decided to pursue his predecessor’s plans to strengthen the Spanish armies which were about to face Napoleon’s attack from the line of the Ebro. His full forces arrived too late to do so.
