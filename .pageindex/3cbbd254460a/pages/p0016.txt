From the human perspective draught animals can be regarded as living machines that
convert chemical energy into motive energy. The chemical energy is the energy of the fodder
they consume.31 All fodder consumed by the animal, as well as the energy needed for rest, is
from the human perspective a necessary cost for the draught work of the animal. The fodder
consumed in a workday is dependent on the size of the animal and how hard they worked.
Fodder requirements for a day at rest are simply dependent on the size of the animal. To
calculate the primary energy of the fodder consumed by draught animals there is thus a need
for information on the numbers of draught animals, how many days they worked during one
year and how much they ate when they worked and when they rested. It is very difficult to
find reliable information on the number of workdays, so we refrain from taking number of
workdays into account. Instead we apply the simple formula for primary energy
consumption by draught animals of equation (1):

Ei = Htt* Hft + Ct * Cft + Dt* Dft

Where:
