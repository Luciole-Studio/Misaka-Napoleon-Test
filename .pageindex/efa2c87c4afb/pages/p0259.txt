Sack’s abrupt transfer from the Rhineland, where he enjoyed popularity,
further depressed public opinion. This could best be described as one of
sullen resignation in the face of requisitioning burdens and isolated but
well-publicised outrages committed by soldiers. Grumbling about taxes
continued following Napoleon’s defeat, when people expected a return
to the ﬁscal policies of the prince-bishops.10 Prussian prestige grew after
Frederick William’s constitutional promises and Waterloo – the authorities
circulated hundreds of patriotic prints immediately following the victory –
