# Is the Industrial Revolution an Existence Proof for 10× AI Growth?

Analysis and charts for the blog post [Is the Industrial Revolution an Existence Proof for 10× AI Growth?](https://mattsclancy.github.io/2026/07/27/industrial-revolution-growth.html)

Some AI optimists cite the Industrial Revolution as a precedent for 10× faster economic growth. This project examines what the historical record actually shows, using the Maddison Project Database 2023 to compare the IR growth shock with the modern distribution of growth rates. All growth rates are computed as compound annual growth rates (geometric means).

## Data

File

Source

mpd2023_web.xlsx

[Maddison Project Database 2023](https://www.rug.nl/ggdc/historicaldevelopment/maddison/releases/maddison-project-database-2023), sheet "Full data"

GDP per capita is in 2011 USD; population is in thousands.

## Reproducing the analysis

### Dependencies

pip install pandas numpy matplotlib openpyxl

Python 3.9+.

### Generating the charts

python3 analysis.py

Output PNGs are saved to output/.

## Key findings

- Pre-IR UK compound growth rate (CAGR, 1252–1652): 0.07%/yr. The settled post-IR rate (1850–1913): 1.02%/yr — a 15× acceleration.
- About 46% of pre-IR UK years exceeded 10× the compound growth rate (0.66%/yr).
- Eight modern analogue countries (GDP $800–$2,500, pop 1–10M, growth −0.3% to 0.9%): compound mean 0.35%/yr. About 19% of their country-years exceeded 10× that rate (3.5%/yr).
- The IR shock in standard deviation terms: 0.17 SDs of the analogue distribution.
- Applying the same shock to the US frontier (1.9%/yr CAGR) yields about 2.8%/yr — not 10×.
- A genuine 10× frontier acceleration (1.9% → 18.9%) would be 3.1 SDs above the mean using the analogue SD, or 7.5 SDs using the US SD.
- Only 0.8% of country-years in the modern era exceeded 18.9%/yr; nearly all are oil windfalls or post-conflict rebounds.
